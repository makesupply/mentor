# Founder Context

The mentor reads this file at the start of every session. It belongs to the founder — edit it freely, keep it honest, and update it when reality changes. The mentor creates `state/founder-context.md` from this template during **Intake** and fills it in with you.

Last updated: [date]

## Who
[Name or handle. Solo, co-founders, investors, a board? What fills their day. Other income streams or obligations that shape available hours. Working style in one or two sentences.]

## The goal
[The number and the date. "First $X in revenue by Y" beats "grow the business." The founder defines success — revenue, profit, ARR, exit, lifestyle business — and the mentor's mission is that definition, not activity, not infrastructure elegance.]

## The business(es)
[One entry per venture: what it sells, business model (DTC product / SaaS / services or agency / marketplace / content or audience / local / other — this tag controls which benchmarks apply), current stage (see `references/stage-playbooks.md`; pre-revenue → validation-playbook), current revenue, and which ONE is the current focus. If more than one venture is listed, the focus line is mandatory — the mentor will enforce it.]

**Current stage:** [set at Intake; the mentor updates this line when a stage gate is crossed]

## Directness calibration
[Set at Intake, adjustable any time. This mentor works by direct challenge — consent and intensity are recorded here, not assumed. Intensity: N/10. Off-limits topics, if any. Example: "9/10 — do not soften findings; family time is out of scope."]

## Tendencies to watch
[The founder's honest failure patterns, written bluntly — the mentor will use these without softening. Filled during Intake, updated as sessions reveal more. Examples of the form: "starts new projects when the current one gets hard", "confuses research with progress", "underprices out of fear", "builds systems instead of selling".]

## Strengths to lean on
[Real, specific advantages: skills, audience, capital, network, tooling, domain knowledge.]

## Operating boundaries
[What the mentor's advice must respect: hours protected for family or health, money that is not riskable, lines the founder will not cross. Advice is never authority — execution decisions stay with the founder.]
