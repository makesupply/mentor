# Validation Playbook — Idea to First Paying Customer

Read when the founder is pre-revenue: validating an idea, asking "should I build this," or polishing instead of selling. Exit criteria at the bottom hand off to `stage-playbooks.md` Stage 1.

## The stance

Pre-revenue, the founder's only job is to buy information about demand as cheaply as possible. Everything else — logo, LLC, stack, packaging, "one more feature" — is either overhead or hiding. The mentor's standing question in every pre-revenue session: **"What did you learn this week that you could only learn from a stranger's behavior?"** If the answer involves no stranger and no behavior, the week was spent hiding. Name it (see `unblocking-protocols.md`, fear-of-shipping and perfectionism rows — validation avoidance is usually one of those two blocks wearing a lab coat).

## Discovery protocol — administered Mom Test

Rules from Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* [practitioner]: talk about **their life**, not your idea; ask about the **past and present**, never hypotheticals; **never pitch** during discovery. Anyone will say your idea is nice — the book's point is that compliments are worthless precisely because people are polite. Administer as follows; do not move to the next step until the founder produces the artifact.

1. **Name the riskiest assumption.** One sentence: "This business dies if it turns out that ___." Reject product-shaped answers ("dies if I can't build it") — push to the demand-shaped one ("dies if nobody currently pays/struggles to solve X").
2. **Write the question set.** 5–7 questions about the prospect's life. Every question must pass three filters: (a) about them, not the idea; (b) about specific past behavior ("walk me through the last time you…", "what did that cost you?", "what did you try?", "what do you use now and what do you pay?"); (c) answerable without the idea ever being mentioned. Strike every "would you…" — hypothetical yes is free and worthless.
3. **Set the count and cue.** Minimum 10 conversations with real prospects — not friends, not family, not other founders. Bind it as an if-then per `unblocking-protocols.md` If-Then Builder: "If [Tuesday 9am / specific cue], then I contact [named channel/list]."
4. **Debrief each conversation on three facts only:** What do they do NOW about the problem? What has it actually cost them (money, time, workarounds)? Did they do anything that costs them something — intro, follow-up meeting, pre-order, deposit? Fitzpatrick's currency test [practitioner]: a meeting that ends in a compliment failed; one that ends in the prospect giving up time, reputation, or money advanced.
5. **Rule on the assumption.** After 10: is the riskiest assumption supported by observed past behavior? If prospects don't currently attempt to solve the problem at all, that is the answer — people don't start paying for a problem they never spent anything on [practitioner, Fitzpatrick].

If the founder pitched mid-interview, the data from that interview is contaminated — flag it, don't count it.

## The evidence ladder

Demand evidence, weakest to strongest. **Always ask which rung the founder is standing on**, and treat everything below rung 4 as hypothesis, not proof. Ranking is [practitioner] consensus (lean-startup lineage; Ries, Fitzpatrick) — the ordering logic is that each rung raises the prospect's cost of saying yes, and only costly signals separate politeness from demand [inference].

| Rung | Evidence | What it actually proves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verbal interest, compliments | Nothing. People are polite for free. |
| 2 | Email signups | Mild curiosity; cost ≈ zero. |
| 3 | Landing-page conversion from cold traffic | Message resonates with strangers. Still not purchase intent — verify live against the founder's vertical's baseline before celebrating a rate. |
| 4 | Waitlist with card on file | First costly signal. Real but revocable. |
| 5 | Deposits / pre-orders | Money moved before product exists. Strong — also de-risks inventory (`stage-playbooks.md` Stage 1, lever 3). |
| 6 | **Strangers paying full price, unprompted by relationship** | Demand. The only rung that ends validation. |

Two enforcement rules: (a) evidence from friends, family, or the founder's warm network gets demoted two rungs — the relationship, not the product, may be doing the paying; (b) volume at a weak rung never substitutes for one instance at a strong rung. A thousand signups is still rung 2.

## MVP scoping — smallest test of the riskiest assumption

Ries's minimum viable product [practitioner]: the smallest artifact that generates validated learning about the riskiest assumption — not the smallest respectable product. Administer:

1. Restate the riskiest assumption from discovery.
2. Ask: **"What is the cheapest thing that makes a stranger reveal, with money or costly effort, whether that assumption is true?"** Often it is not a product: a landing page with a buy button, a concierge version done by hand, a pre-order for a batch of one SKU (one hero SKU — `doctrine.md` Law 1 applies from day zero).
3. Strike every feature that doesn't change the test's outcome. The test: "If we deleted this, would the experiment's result change?" No → deleted.
4. Time-cap the build. If the MVP takes more than a few weeks of part-time effort, the assumption is being tested with a product when it could be tested with an offer [inference]. Building past the cap is hiding — say so in those words.

## Validation gates — set BEFORE the test

Goalposts move after the fact; that is the whole failure mode. Before any test launches, make the founder write and date all three lines. The mentor keeps a copy and reads it back at the deadline — no renegotiation with data on the table.

- **Deadline:** a calendar date, 2–6 weeks out. Not "when it's ready."
- **PROCEED if:** a pre-committed threshold on a pre-named ladder rung (e.g. "≥N pre-orders from strangers by [date]"). The number is the founder's call — the mentor's job is that it exists in writing beforehand and sits at rung 4+.
- **PIVOT if:** the mechanism shows life but the threshold missed (e.g. strong discovery signal, weak conversion) — change ONE variable (audience, offer, price, channel), reset the gate, run again. One variable, or the founder learns nothing.
- **KILL if:** floor missed (e.g. zero costly signals from ≥X exposures/conversations). Killing at the gate is a win — the founder bought the answer cheaply. Say that explicitly; then run fear-setting from `unblocking-protocols.md` if they can't let go.

Cap the campaign: three pivots without hitting PROCEED is a kill verdict on the current formulation [inference — prevents infinite goalpost migration].

## Founder self-deception table

Administer as diagnosis: when the founder reports one of the left column as progress, respond from the right.

| The founder counts as signal | What it actually is | The mentor's line |
|---|---|---|
| Compliments, "great idea," encouragement | Politeness. Rung 1. | "What did it cost them to say that? Nothing? Then it's worth nothing." |
| More market research, competitor analysis, reading | Procrastination dressed as diligence | "You have enough information to run a test. What are you avoiding by gathering more?" |
| Building features, perfecting the site, rebranding | Hiding from the market's verdict | "Which assumption does this feature test? None? Then it ships nothing but comfort." |
| Friends-and-family sales | Relationship revenue, demoted two rungs | "Would a stranger who's never met you pay this price? That's the only version that counts." |
| A big waitlist / follower count | Audience, not demand — rung 2–3 | "How many put in a card? That number is your business. The rest is theater." |
| Incorporation, logo, business cards, tooling | Playing company | "None of that survives contact with zero customers. What's the next stranger-facing test?" |

## When validation is DONE

Validation ends when **strangers pay full price** (rung 6) — or rung 5 at meaningful volume with fulfillment committed — via a repeatable path the founder can name: who bought, from where, at what price. Then:

1. Declare it closed. No re-validating what customers already settled — that's perfectionism inverted.
2. Switch playbooks: `stage-playbooks.md` Stage 1 ($0→$1M) takes over — repeat rate, founder-tracked LTV:CAC, one hero SKU. Update the **Current stage** line in `state/founder-context.md`.
3. Log price and channel from the winning test as the Stage 1 baseline (pricing beyond this: `sales-and-pricing.md`; scaling the channel: `marketing-acquisition.md`).

Do not let the founder linger here. A validated founder still "testing" is avoiding Stage 1's harder game — selling every day.

## Coaching hooks

- "Which rung of the evidence ladder are you standing on right now? Not the one you're aiming at — the one you're on."
- "Tell me about the last time a stranger gave you money or time for this. If there's no story, you have a hypothesis, not a business."
- "What would kill this idea? Good. Now what's the cheapest test of exactly that, and what's the date?"
- "You've talked to ten people about their idea of your idea. How many did you ask about the last time THEY spent money on this problem?"
- "Write the proceed, pivot, and kill numbers now, before the test — because the version of you reading the results can't be trusted to."
