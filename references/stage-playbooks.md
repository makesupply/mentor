# Stage Playbooks — $0 → $1M → $10M → $100M

Condensed from Report 2 (`references/research/report-2-stage-playbooks.md`), specific to bootstrapped DTC supplements/wellness on Shopify — the skill's deepest-researched vertical. The stage structure, gates, and failure patterns generalize to most product businesses; the dollar benchmarks are vertical-specific. Benchmarks researched Aug 2026 — verify live when a decision hangs on one.

> The founder's current stage is recorded in `state/founder-context.md` (set at Intake, updated when a gate is crossed) — resolve it there at session start. This file is read-only reference content.

For the pre-revenue stage (idea → first dollar), read `references/validation-playbook.md` — Stage 1 below assumes a validated product exists. For non-DTC business models (SaaS, agency, marketplace, creator, local), the stage benchmarks live in `references/stage-playbooks-by-model.md` — never quote this file's numbers to those founders.

## Stage 1: $0 → $1M

**Levers, ranked:** (1) Hero-SKU product-market fit BEFORE paid scaling — repeat rate is the proof, not sales. (2) Founder-run, spreadsheet-tracked paid social (Meta/TikTok) — Ali model. (3) Pre-orders/crowdfunding to validate with zero inventory risk. (4) Direct customer feedback loop (Obvi's 3-question survey) shaping the product. (5) AI-assisted creative volume [uncertain — industry-wide shift, no named-brand primary source].

**Numbers that matter:**
| Metric | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| Supplement CAC | $53–78 |
| LTV:CAC | ≥3:1 (below 2:1 = unprofitable) |
| Median DTC AOV (2025) | ~$74 |
| Contribution margin | 15–20% median; top quartile 28%+ |
| CAC payback | 6–9 months median; <3 top quartile |
| 12-mo supplement subscriber LTV | $300–600 |
| First-month subscription churn | 20–30% (normal — don't panic) |
| First-reorder rate | 86.6% — lowest of the five wellness verticals Recharge measured; THE cliff to engineer against |

Underwrite acquisition against 12-month subscriber LTV, not first-order margin. Expect thin/negative first-order contribution.

**How brands die here:** scaling spend before repeat rate is fixed (Native's melting formula almost killed it); single-channel dependency baked in from day one (76% of the DTC Graveyard's 50 dead brands).

**Team:** founder personally runs acquisition; first hire (if any) is a marketing generalist, not customer service [practitioner frameworks conflict — the McKinsey claim is secondhand via an agency blog]. Ali ran to $25M+ with 8 people. With an AI workforce, Stage 1 headcount can be zero.

**Finance:** no debt/RBF yet. Exploit the structural gift: subscription prepay → negative cash conversion cycle (-15 to -60 days). Pure-DTC benchmark 30–60 days.

**Retention:** win the first reorder — product efficacy, onboarding email/SMS, flexible skip options. That single cliff is most of the Stage 1 retention war.

## Stage 2: $1M → $10M

**Levers, ranked:** (1) Creator seeding at scale (Ridge: 5,000+ sponsored). (2) Brand-distinct/comedic creative replacing generic DR ads (True Classic's unlock). (3) Engineered scarcity and drop-based restocks (Obvi: each restock ~2x prior). (4) Subscription-default pricing architecture (AG1: $79 sub < $99 one-time → 75% adoption). (5) Retail as secondary, non-dominant channel (Obvi: 80/20 DTC/retail).

**Numbers that matter:** supplement monthly churn 5–8% (health/wellness 8.8% per Recharge 2026 — hardest-churning replenishment category); deep-lifecycle active churn 17.3%/cycle with 6.9% skip rate — skips keep subscribers, push skips not cancels; annual billing cuts effective churn 60–80% vs monthly; CAC inflation is structural ($34→$57 avg 2021→2024 across the failure cohort) — plan for it, don't be surprised by it.

**Stop doing:** treating Meta as the only channel; deferring retention infrastructure ("still in acquisition mode" is how the Graveyard brands died); gut-feel inventory (True Classic's $40M lesson — this stage is where it bites).

**Team:** founder exits day-to-day ad buying; adds ops lead + retention/email specialist; demand planning becomes a role or a rigorous process.

**Finance:** CCC discipline is make-or-break. RBF (repaid from revenue, typically capped 1.3–3x) appropriately bridges inventory gaps once revenue is consistent. Debt before equity.

## Stage 3: $10M → $100M

**Levers, ranked:** (1) Retention infrastructure as a PRIMARY lever (AG1's $711 LTV machine). (2) Equity-based creator/podcast partnerships, not pure media buys. (3) Category/channel expansion only after core retention economics are proven (Ridge's wedding bands: 8 figures year one). (4) Professionalize leadership (Francis→Hewitt tripled revenue; AG1→Cole). (5) Structured debt over equity to preserve ownership (Gymshark sold 21% only at £1B+, founder kept 70%+).

**Numbers that matter:** prepaid-subscription DTC can hold negative CCC even at $50M–$200M+; mature public DTC/CPG median CCC 130 days (10-K data) — subscription is the structural edge; annual-billing supplements ~96% NRR at 12 months vs ~70% monthly; channel mix diversifies hard post-2025 (BFCM 2025: Meta +1.4% YoY vs Google +23%, AppLovin +114%, Snap +58%).

**Stop doing:** scaling spend without retention investment (Allbirds: $4.1B valuation → $39M revenue in 53 months; Casper; Bonobos); founder-intuition inventory; leaving subscription-vs-one-time pricing unengineered.

**Team:** founder fully out of media buying and CX; capital allocation, category strategy, and hiring/becoming a professional CEO. Leadership layer: CMO, supply chain, HR above ~$15M.

## Stage-transition gates

**Stage 1 → 2, ready when:** repeat/second-order rate trending UP (Native precedent); LTV:CAC ≥ ~3:1 on a founder-tracked weekly sheet; one paid channel proven profitable at small budget; the founder's personal bottleneck function identified and delegation begun.

**Stage 2 → 3, ready when:** subscription/repeat revenue share growing with churn trending ≤5–8%; inventory planning is a documented process, not gut; a second channel contributes double-digit % of revenue; CCC measured and trending to benchmark (30–60 days, or negative on prepay).
