# Blocker Map — Differential Diagnosis of What's Stopping the Founder

Read in Unblock mode BEFORE choosing an intervention: diagnose here, then administer from `references/unblocking-protocols.md`. Condensed from Report 8 (`references/research/report-8-mental-blockers.md`, 62 sources). Replication status is stated because famous psychology findings died in replication — never coach from a dead one.

## The differential table — start here

| Founder says... | Likely blocker | First diagnostic question |
|---|---|---|
| "I need it to be perfect before I ship" | Perfectionism (socially prescribed concerns) | "Perfect according to whom — you, or someone imagined watching?" |
| "I keep opening the doc and closing it again" | Procrastination (mood-repair avoidance) | "What feeling shows up right before you close it?" |
| "Any day now they'll realize I don't know what I'm doing" | Impostor phenomenon | "What would 'being found out' actually cost you — concretely?" |
| "I didn't really prepare for that pitch" | Self-handicapping (fear of failure) | "If you had prepared fully and it still failed, what would that mean about you?" |
| "If this works, everyone will expect more from me" | Fear of success | "What does 'more' feel like it would take away from you?" |
| "I have twelve tabs open and lose track constantly" | Executive-function overload | "What's the smallest external system that could hold this for you?" |
| "I keep replaying that investor call at 2am" | Rumination/worry | "Is the replaying solving something, or just repeating?" |
| "I'd rather build features than make that follow-up call" | Rejection sensitivity/avoidance | "What's the actual worst realistic outcome of that call?" |
| "By 4pm I just agree to everything" | Decision friction (mechanism contested) | "How many decisions today had unclear criteria going in?" |
| "I should be further along — I'm such an idiot" | Self-criticism (low self-compassion) | "What would you say to a friend in your exact position?" |
| "I'm just not a numbers person" | Limiting belief / fixed frame | "Is that a fact about you, or a story about a skill you haven't built yet?" |

## The ten blockers — evidence, one line of mechanism, and the lever

**1. Procrastination.** Voluntary delay despite expecting to be worse off; the mood-repair model says avoidance is emotion regulation — relief now, shame later, shame feeds the next delay [peer-reviewed, Steel 2007 meta-analysis (691 correlations); Sirois & Pychyl]. Interventions work: RCT meta-analysis g = 1.18 [peer-reviewed] — if-then plans, task chunking below the aversiveness threshold, and self-forgiveness (the shame is fuel for the cycle, not for the work). Lever: If-Then Builder + Self-Compassion Reset (`unblocking-protocols.md`).

**2. Perfectionism.** Two subtypes pull OPPOSITE directions [peer-reviewed, Hewitt & Flett; Sirois et al. 2017 meta-analysis, N≈19,500]: perfectionistic CONCERNS (fear of others' judgment) drive procrastination (r=+0.23); perfectionistic STRIVINGS (own high standards) protect against it (r=−0.22). Coach accordingly: preserve the standards, attack the imagined audience. Cohort data says socially prescribed perfectionism is rising and accelerating [peer-reviewed, Curran & Hill, 83k students]. Lever: WOOP on the internal obstacle + the "perfect according to whom" question.

**3. Impostor phenomenon.** Persistent self-perceived fraudulence despite objective success; explicitly NOT a clinical syndrome [peer-reviewed, Clance & Imes 1978]. Founder-specific: 77% of 226 surveyed entrepreneurs experienced it, and it predicted WORSE venture performance — mitigated by peer/network support [peer-reviewed, AOM 2024]. Honest efficacy note: a coaching trial showed only modest gains vs control — context and peers move this more than solo reframing does [peer-reviewed]. Lever: name the cycle (over-prepare → discount success → repeat), prescribe founder-peer contact (the +50% resilience finding in `unblocking-protocols.md` Wellbeing Floor), and make them state the evidence for competence out loud.

**4. Fear of failure / fear of success.** Fear of failure has five distinct faces [peer-reviewed, Conroy's PFAI]: shame, self-devaluation, uncertain future, others losing interest, upsetting others — diagnose WHICH, don't treat it as one thing. Self-handicapping is its behavioral fingerprint: manufacturing an excuse before the test (not preparing, late nights, new side project) protects the story "I could have" [peer-reviewed, Berglas & Jones 1978] — and reliably worsens actual performance. Fear of success is weakly operationalized [contested/practitioner] — treat as identity-load question, not a construct. Lever: fear-setting + reframe the launch as a test of the market, not a referendum on the founder.

**5. Executive function / ADHD-type overload.** The honest arc of the Wiklund research line, including his own 2026 meta-analytic self-correction (298 effect sizes): ADHD symptoms push people INTO entrepreneurship (intention ~1.7x, venturing ~2x) but total symptoms are NEGATIVELY linked to post-launch outcomes; hyperactivity/impulsivity predicts entrepreneurial behavior, not results; inattention hurts results [peer-reviewed]. The "ADHD advantage" is conditional at best — never romanticize it. Coaching scaffolds are practitioner-tier: externalize memory (checklists, single visible task list), cut task-initiation friction, structured accountability — this skill's ledger and tripwires ARE that scaffolding. Formal diagnosis or treatment questions → referral (see boundary).

**6. Rumination and worry.** Repetitive dwelling prolongs and biases — it predicts depression onset and impairs problem-solving [peer-reviewed, Nolen-Hoeksema tradition]. The best-supported non-clinical tool is stimulus control: a scheduled daily worry window, worries deferred to it rather than suppressed [peer-reviewed technique, Borkovec 1983]. Lever: Worry Window script (`unblocking-protocols.md`) + convert any solvable worry into an if-then or a tripwire (`references/foresight-protocols.md` — a written tripwire is a worry with a job).

**7. Rejection sensitivity / ask-avoidance.** Sales-call reluctance is experiential avoidance: skipping the call removes discomfort now, reinforcing the loop [practitioner; construct from peer-reviewed relational research]. Rejection activates pain-overlapping neural pathways — it genuinely hurts; say so, then build the ladder. Lever: Ask Ladder (graded exposure — warm → lukewarm → cold) + rejection-as-data reframe (`sales-and-pricing.md`). Trauma-rooted avoidance → referral, not exposure coaching.

**8. Decision friction ("decision fatigue").** The resource story is DEAD: ego depletion failed preregistered replication across 23 labs (d ≈ 0.04) and a proponent-designed retest [peer-reviewed]; choice-overload meta-analysis averages near zero with real heterogeneity [peer-reviewed, contested]. NEVER say "your willpower is depleted." What survives mechanism-agnostically: fewer and clearer decisions help — defaults, pre-decision (the Foresight tripwires are pre-decisions), delegation, decision criteria set before the day starts. Lever: count the decisions with unclear criteria; give the top one a rule.

**9. Self-criticism vs self-compassion.** The founder's fear — "if I'm kind to myself I'll lose my edge" — is empirically backwards: induced self-compassion after failure INCREASED motivation to improve versus self-esteem-boosting or distraction [peer-reviewed, Breines & Chen]; self-compassion correlates with self-efficacy (medium, 60-study meta-analysis) and predicts mastery goals over approval-seeking [peer-reviewed, Neff]. Interventions reduce self-criticism at g = 0.51 [peer-reviewed, 20 RCTs]. Lever: Self-Compassion Reset script — this is a performance intervention, frame it exactly that way.

**10. Limiting beliefs / mindset.** Report honestly: mindset-achievement correlation is r ≈ 0.10, intervention effects d ≈ 0.08, shrinking to non-significant after bias correction [peer-reviewed, Sisk et al. 2018; Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023]. What survives: small real effects for STRUGGLING people in SUPPORTIVE environments [peer-reviewed, Yeager 2019, N=12,490]. So: never sell "adopt a growth mindset" as a lever. DO attack specific fixed self-stories ("not a numbers person") as skill-gaps with a first rep — that's just accurate, and it routes to `finance-fundamentals.md` or `sales-and-pricing.md` rather than to belief work.

## Cascades — blockers compound

- **Perfectionist concerns → procrastination → shame → more avoidance.** Break it at the shame link (self-compassion), not the standards link [peer-reviewed chain].
- **Impostor feelings → self-handicapping → avoidant hiring and under-scaling.** "We never really tried" protects the ability story [peer-reviewed + inferred]. Watch for it in Decision Gate.
- **Fear of shame ≈ rejection sensitivity in sales** — same avoidance, different construct; the differential question is whether the dread is about the outcome or the "no" itself.
- **EF overload → open loops → 2am rumination.** The fix is upstream: external capture systems, not sleep advice.

## Referral boundary — beyond coaching, say so plainly

Coach the pattern; refer the impairment. Referral triggers (`SKILL.md` Boundaries + `unblocking-protocols.md` Wellbeing Floor): persistent depressive symptoms with functional impairment beyond work; possible clinical ADHD (scaffold EF, never diagnose or advise on treatment); panic-level or generalized anxiety (worry postponement is an adjunct, not a substitute); ANY self-handicapping via alcohol/substances (the original Berglas & Jones paradigm — referral, immediately); trauma-linked avoidance (exposure without clinical oversight risks harm); anything touching safety. The operating rule: performance friction inside the business is coachable; impairment that follows the founder home — sleep, relationships, health, safety — gets a professional, and the mentor keeps helping with the business alongside.
