## Dossiers

**Ben Francis — Gymshark (apparel).** £1,000 → £646M (FY2025), bootstrapped until a 21% stake sale at £1B+ (2020). Started as a dropshipping supplements site that took 6 weeks to make one sale; pivoted to sewing fitted gymwear in the garage. Seeded micro-YouTubers with free gear and equity before "influencer marketing" existed. Stepped down as CEO at 23 for a Reebok veteran (revenue tripled), returned later. Frames success as "layering thoughtful, patient actions over a decade." Never wholesale — protected DTC margins and customer data.

**Sean Frank — Ridge (EDC accessories).** $266K Kickstarter → $100M by 2021 → multi-hundred-million; zero outside equity, uses debt deliberately: "we can think a decade out." 80% gross margin single hero SKU; 5,000+ sponsored influencers; now 200+ new ad variations monthly. Hardest year 2018–19: inventory out-of-stock plus surprise tax bill at $15–18M. Expanded into "boring" categories competitors dismissed — wedding bands did eight figures in year one. Treats the CEO role as a skill, not a founder entitlement.

**Moiz Ali — Native (deodorant).** ~$500/month (July 2015) → $100M P&G exit (Nov 2017), ~$500K raised, 90%+ ownership at exit. PR failed, Google Ads failed; won on Facebook with a personal spreadsheet tracking every ad's CTR/CPA/ROI. Fixed the melting-formula defect → repeat rate 20%→50%, rating 3.8→4.7. Ran media buying himself; 8 employees at $25–35M. Post-acquisition A/B test: his original formula beat P&G's lab reformulation.

**Ryan Bartlett — True Classic (menswear).** $3K pooled among 3 co-founders (2019) → $150M by 2022 → $250M pace. Ex-poker pro; product insight: t-shirts fitted for "the everyman" body. Comedic video ads over generic direct response. Near-death: gut-feel $40M inventory over-order — "the stupidest decision we ever made," 18 months to dig out, fixed with professional demand planners. Resisted women's/kids' lines 5+ years for focus. Coaches teams to a weekly top-3-impact focus; keeps a 9–3 personal schedule at $200M+ scale.

**Sara Blakely — Spanx (shapewear).** $5,000 → ~$400M revenue, zero outside capital for 21 years, $1.2B Blackstone deal (2021). Wrote her own patent from a library book; landed Neiman Marcus by modeling the product in the bathroom; Oprah gift basket → Favorite Things (unpaid) → $4M→$10M year two. No traditional advertising for 16 years. Kept the idea silent for a year: "ideas are most vulnerable the moment you have them." Risk style: bounded, never debt, reinvested profit.

**Peter Rahal — RXBAR (CPG bars).** Parents' basement → $130M net revenue → $600M Kellogg exit (2017) with 75 employees. Launched into CrossFit gyms where clean-label positioning resonated. The contrarian move that "really changed the business": full ingredient list on the FRONT of the pack ("No B.S.") when the category buried it on the back.

**Mike Salguero — ButcherBox (meat subscription).** $275K (2015 Kickstarter) → $105M (2018) → ~$600M; zero VC ever. Prior company raised $30M and failed — lesson: "cater to customers, not investors." Converted Kickstarter backers into recurring subscribers in a category (meat) nobody had made subscription. Discipline: cash flow and unit economics over top-line; takes Fridays off; had a down year in '23 and refocused on unit economics.

**Ronak Shah — Obvi (collagen supplements).** $10K → $40M in ~5 years, 8-person team, bootstrapped to ~$30M. All three co-founders were performance marketers first. 37–40% monthly retention compounding; three-question Typeform drives product decisions ("ask them what they want, then deliver it"). Engineered scarcity: deliberate stockouts, restocks doubling prior launches. Subscription-platform switch alone: +30% revenue in 90 days. Closest analog for any bootstrapped consumable-supplement founder.

**Chris Ashenden / Kat Cole — AG1 (greens supplement).** Bootstrapped ~12 years to ~$150M run-rate; $600M revenue 2024. ONE SKU for over a decade. Subscription priced BELOW one-time ($79 vs $99) → ~75% subscription adoption. Eight-email onboarding, formal skip/swap/pause/win-back cancellation defense → LTV ~$711 vs ~$213 industry. Creator partnerships paid partly in equity (Rogan, Huberman). Governance warning: founder's concealed past conviction surfaced in 2024 and forced his resignation — integrity debts compound too.

**Mike Cessario — Liquid Death (beverage).** $1,500 mock ad before a product existed → $333M (2024); the cohort's VC-heavy outlier (~$264M+ raised) — included for the marketing playbook, not the capital path. Entertainment-first brand ("we don't take ourselves seriously"), converted viral attention into 113K+ retail doors.

**Jake Kassan — MVMT (watches).** $5K credit-card debt + $300K Indiegogo → $1M/$7M/$30M/$60–75M in four years → $100M+ Movado exit at 27. Alibaba direct sourcing to break the wholesale markup; Facebook ads + influencers; rejected brick-and-mortar. THE WARNING: after the exit — "it'd never been more miserable." Identity fused with the business complicates life after it. Build a self that survives the outcome.

**Danny Winer — HexClad (cookware).** ~$6M → "above $600 million" (2025, his words); celebrity partnership-driven (Ramsay-era). Thinnest documentation in the cohort — use only the two verified frames: hero-product acquisition + retention + Q4 seasonal planning ("plant the seeds early"), and the revenue scale itself.
