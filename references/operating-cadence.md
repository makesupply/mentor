# Operating Cadence — The Founder Rhythm

Read when setting up or auditing how the founder runs their day, week, quarter, and energy, and every time Weekly Review mode runs. The calendar is the strategy: whatever the founder's stated priority, the cadence reveals the real one.

## Daily — the constraint block

One deep-work block on the current constraint (stage-playbooks.md names it) BEFORE any reactive work — before email, Slack, dashboards, or "quick checks." Reactive work expands to fill whatever it is given; the constraint never volunteers for attention.

Maker vs manager schedule [practitioner — Paul Graham]: maker work needs half-day units; a single meeting in the middle destroys the unit. Managers run in hour slots. A solo founder is both people and must schedule as both — maker mornings, manager afternoons, never interleaved.

Administer, don't advise:
1. "What is the current constraint?" If they can't name it in one sentence, stop — re-diagnose before scheduling anything (stage-playbooks.md).
2. "What time tomorrow is the block, and how long?" Reject vague answers; demand clock time, minimum 90 minutes.
3. "What happens to the phone and the inbox during it?" No mechanism = no block.
4. Bind it as an if-then (unblocking-protocols.md, If-Then Builder): "If it's [time], then I open [the constraint work] before anything else."

## Weekly operating review

Run in this order — after the ledger audit, which SKILL.md's session-start protocol has already run before this table begins. Make the founder answer each step before moving on — a skipped step is the tell.

| Step | Make them answer | Fail condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-3 | "What were the three highest-impact tasks this week? Done or not?" [practitioner — Ryan Bartlett coaches teams on top-3 highest-impact tasks weekly] | More than 3 named = no prioritization happened |
| 2. Stage-gate metrics | The current stage's gate numbers, week-over-week (stage-playbooks.md) | "I'd have to check" — at Stage 1 the founder owns the numbers personally (doctrine.md Law 3) |
| 3. Ledger audit | Each open ledger commitment: kept, broken, or renegotiated — say which, out loud | Silent quiet-dropping of a commitment |
| 4. Boundary check | "What did you NOT do this week because you protected it?" (unblocking-protocols.md, burnout row) | "Nothing" = no boundaries, just a slogan |
| 5. Focus accounting | The hours ratio below | Founder estimates instead of counting |
| 6. Next top-3 | Three tasks for next week, each tied to the constraint, each with an if-then | A task that serves comfort, not the constraint |

## Focus accounting — the ratio is the diagnosis

Have the founder bucket last week's actual working hours honestly:

| Bucket | Definition |
|---|---|
| Revenue-earning | Work that moves the current constraint on the thing that earns (or will earn) money — shipping, selling, talking to customers, fixing retention |
| Infrastructure | Systems, tooling, automation, dashboards, reorganizing |
| Busywork | Feels like work, moves nothing: inbox grooming, dashboard-watching, tweaking done things |
| New shiny | Any hours on a project that is not the current business |

Read the ratio, not the total. Rules of thumb [inference — no published benchmark; state the reasoning, verify against results]:
- Revenue-earning under ~half of hours: the week's diagnosis is allocation, not effort. Stop discussing tactics until it's fixed.
- Infrastructure exceeding revenue-earning for two consecutive weeks: name it. Systems-building is the most productive-feeling form of procrastination available to a systems thinker — it produces artifacts, dopamine, and zero customers. The test: "What revenue-earning action was this system supposed to unblock, and did that action happen?"
- Any "new shiny" hours before the current product retains customers: doctrine.md Law 1 violation. Run the shiny-object row in unblocking-protocols.md.

## Monthly / quarterly

1. **Re-diagnose the constraint.** Constraints move; cadences fossilize. Ask: "Is the thing the daily block points at still the bottleneck, or did you just get comfortable working on it?"
2. **Doctrine audit.** Walk the laws (doctrine.md) against the last quarter's actual decisions. Name each violation with its law number and the founder it came from.
3. **Schedule the deload.** After any 4–6-week heavy push, put a deliberately lighter week ON THE CALENDAR now (unblocking-protocols.md, Deload Cadence). Unscheduled recovery doesn't happen; unplanned recovery is called burnout.
4. **Portfolio honesty.** Every side project gets a verdict: kill / pause / keep. "Pause" requires a written re-entry condition ("I reopen this when X metric hits Y") — otherwise it's a kill the founder is too sentimental to say. More than one "keep" besides the main business needs justification against Law 1.

## Annual

1. **Strategy reset.** One session, three questions: What business is this actually, based on where the money came from? What would make next year's constraint disappear? What are we explicitly NOT doing next year? Write the not-doing list down; it is the strategy.
2. **The identity question.** Kassan's warning (founder-dossiers.md): identity fused to the business made a $100M exit "never been more miserable." Once a year, ask directly: "If this business ended next year — sold, failed, either — who are you on Tuesday morning?" A founder who can't answer is building a trap, not a company. Build a self that survives the outcome: training, family, craft, anything with roots outside the P&L.

## Decision hygiene

- **Decision journal** [practitioner — standard tool in investing/decision practice]: for every consequential call, log four fields at decision time: the decision, the date, the expected outcome, and a review date. At the review date, compare outcome to expectation IN WRITING before memory rewrites the story. The journal exists to catch bad process that got lucky and good process that got unlucky.
- **Reversible fast, irreversible slow** [practitioner — Bezos's two-way vs one-way doors]: if it can be undone cheaply, deciding slowly is the expensive choice — decide with ~70% of the information and move. If it can't (lease, hire, large inventory buy, brand repositioning, equity), it earns the journal, a sleep, and a fear-setting pass (unblocking-protocols.md) when stakes are high. The most common founder error is running one-way-door process on two-way doors — thoroughness as avoidance.
- Consequential + irreversible + owner-level is also where the founder should not decide alone (unblocking-protocols.md, isolation row).

## Energy as a metric

Sleep, training, and recovery are performance infrastructure, not rewards for shipping. The direction is robust [peer-reviewed — sleep restriction reliably degrades attention, working memory, and decision quality; magnitudes vary by study, keep claims modest]: an under-slept founder is a worse decision-maker running the same calendar and calling it discipline.

Track it like a stage-gate metric, weekly, alongside the business numbers: hours slept, training sessions completed, and one subjective 1–10 energy score. Two weeks of decline is a leading indicator — cut load or schedule the deload before performance forces the issue. Chronic strain beyond that: Wellbeing Floor, unblocking-protocols.md. Reviewing energy only after output drops is checking the fuel gauge after the engine stops.

## Coaching hooks

- "Show me last week's calendar. Not your priorities — your calendar. That's the real priority list."
- "How many hours last week touched the thing that earns money? Count, don't estimate."
- "You built three systems this month. What revenue-earning action did they unblock, and did it happen?"
- "What did you NOT do this week because you protected it? If the answer is nothing, we start there."
- "Is this a one-way door or a two-way door? Then why are you giving it one-way-door hours?"
