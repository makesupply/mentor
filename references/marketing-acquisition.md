# Marketing & Acquisition — Channel Doctrine

Read when the founder asks where customers will come from, wants to scale spend, proposes a new channel, or is hopping channels. Administer the positioning gate before any channel discussion — no exceptions.

## Positioning gate (run first, every time)

No channel fixes a product nobody can describe. Make the founder complete this sentence out loud, no notes:

> "For [specific person], [product] replaces [what they use now] because [the one reason it wins]."

Administer strictly:
1. **Who** — a person you could find on a list, not "busy professionals." If the founder names two audiences, make them pick one for the next 90 days.
2. **Replaces** — every purchase displaces something, even "doing nothing" or a spreadsheet. If the founder says "there's no competition," that usually means no demand — route to validation-playbook.md.
3. **Why it wins** — one reason, in the customer's words, not the founder's feature list. [Practitioner — positioning discipline per April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome*.]

Fail the gate → stop. Channel talk is procrastination until this sentence is sayable. A founder who can't say it will burn spend proving it on the ad platform's meter.

## Channel selection protocol

Never let the founder pick a channel by vibe or by what a YouTube guru scaled. Score candidates on three factors — make the founder answer each before comparing:

1. **Gathering** — "Where do people who already have this problem congregate TODAY?" Demand named places (a subreddit, a trade show, a search query with volume, a platform feed, a buyer's inbox). "Everyone's on Instagram" is not an answer; verify live.
2. **Unfair advantage** — "What can you do in that place that a funded competitor can't buy?" Founder's face and story, existing audience, craft skill (video, writing, cold outreach), community standing, product that photographs itself. No advantage → the channel is an auction, and the founder is bidding against better balance sheets.
3. **Margin tolerance** — "What can you afford to pay for a customer and still eat?" Contribution margin per order and expected LTV set the CAC ceiling (finance-fundamentals.md for the math; Law 3 for the 3:1 floor). Thin margin disqualifies paid channels outright — organic, referral, and retention are not optional there, they are the model.

The channel that scores on all three wins. Two out of three is a debate; the mentor breaks ties toward the channel with the founder's strongest unfair advantage — advantage compounds, auctions don't.

## Sequencing discipline

**One primary channel at a time, driven to proven.** Two half-worked channels lose to one worked channel — a solo founder's attention is the scarcest input. This does NOT contradict Law 4 (never one channel): Law 4 condemns *ending* at one channel — 76% of the DTC Graveyard's dead brands were single-channel. The doctrine is sequential monogamy: prove channel one, THEN add the second. By $1M–$10M a second proven channel is a stage gate (doctrine.md Law 4, stage-playbooks.md), not a nice-to-have.

**"Proven" means** [inference — define with the founder, verify live]: the channel produces customers at a known, repeatable cost inside the CAC ceiling for several consecutive weeks, from more than one creative/message/tactic — not one viral spike, not one lucky ad. Make the founder write their own proven-threshold BEFORE starting; otherwise they'll declare victory on the first good week or quit on the first bad one.

**Channel-hopping is a mental block, not a strategy.** Third channel in six months → stop administering marketing advice and run the shiny-object row in unblocking-protocols.md. The pattern is almost never "wrong channel" three times; it's leaving before the boring iteration that makes any channel work.

## Organic & creator playbook (the Stage 1–2 lever)

Law 9: Gymshark seeded 50–100k-subscriber fitness YouTubers "when nobody cared." The generalized move — give the product to small, trusted voices in the exact gathering place before the space gets expensive:

1. **List** 50–100 micro-voices the target customer already trusts (creators, newsletter writers, community moderators, podcast hosts, respected practitioners — whatever the niche's equivalent is).
2. **Seed** with product and a personal note. No script demands, no contract at this stage — authenticity is the asset being purchased.
3. **Track** responses in a spreadsheet: who posted, what they said, what it drove. Double down on the segment that converts; expect most seeds to yield nothing.
4. **Escalate** winners into real relationships — paid deals, affiliate terms, equity for the truly aligned (Law 9: AG1, Gymshark). Incentives across years beat campaigns.

**Founder-led content:** at Stage 1–2 the founder IS the brand's cheapest distribution — building in public, teaching what they know, showing the product's making. It compounds, costs time not cash, and produces the raw material paid ads will later need. A founder "too busy" for this while asking for paid-spend approval has the sequence backwards.

**UGC flywheel:** every happy customer is a potential asset. Systematize the ask — post-purchase prompts, reposting with credit, making customers visible. Customer-voice creative is both the organic feed and the best-performing paid raw material [practitioner — standard DTC operating pattern].

## Paid media discipline

Paid amplifies what already works; it never discovers product–market fit. Rules the mentor enforces:

- **Founder-tracked spreadsheet.** Moiz Ali's hand-built ad spreadsheet ran a company that sold for $100M (Law 3). At Stage 1–2 the founder personally logs spend, orders, and CAC per ad, weekly minimum. Platform dashboards grade their own homework; the spreadsheet reconciles to actual bank-account revenue. No delegation, no "the dashboard says."
- **Kill criteria in advance.** Before any ad goes live, make the founder write: "I kill this ad when it has spent $X (roughly 1–2× target CAC [inference — set the exact figure with the founder]) without a sale, or when CAC exceeds $Y for Z days." Deciding mid-flight guarantees sunk-cost drift — the ad that's "about to turn around" is the founder's ego talking.
- **Small-budget proof before scale.** Prove the ad converts at small daily spend before multiplying budget. Scale in steps, not leaps; every increase must survive its own kill criteria. Never scale to hit a revenue goal — scale because unit economics at current spend earned it.
- **Incrementality skepticism.** Standing question: "Would they have bought anyway?" Platforms claim credit for customers organic already won — branded-search and retargeting numbers flatter hardest [practitioner — widely documented attribution inflation; measure, don't assume]. Cheapest honest tests: pause a retargeting campaign for two weeks and watch total revenue, not platform ROAS; compare blended CAC (total marketing spend ÷ total new customers) against the platform's story. When they diverge, believe blended.
- **Creative is the targeting.** On modern auction platforms the message finds the audience; testing ten creatives beats micro-tuning audience settings [practitioner]. Feed the machine the organic/UGC winners — this is why the organic playbook runs first.

## Retention as the acquisition multiplier

A leaky product makes every channel unprofitable — the math is the same in any business. Repeat purchases raise LTV, which raises the affordable CAC ceiling; word-of-mouth adds zero-cost customers, which lowers blended CAC. Compounded, the retained-product founder can outbid everyone in the auction while the leaky-product founder finds every channel "too expensive." The channel was never the problem.

Enforcement: Law 2 — ask for the second-order rate (or renewal/repeat-usage equivalent) before blessing ANY spend increase. Weak retention → the growth budget goes to fixing the product and the post-purchase experience first (Law 5: a shipped retention feature usually beats an ad-spend increase at equal effort). Referral mechanics are bolt-on amplifiers of satisfaction that must already exist — they cannot create it.

## Vanity metrics — what the mentor refuses to accept as progress

| Founder reports | Why it's not progress | What the mentor demands instead |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions / reach / views | Costless to generate; buys nothing | Customers acquired, and cost per |
| Follower count | Audience ≠ buyers; often wrong audience entirely | Conversion of audience to revenue, ever |
| Likes / engagement rate | Applause, not payment | Click-through to purchase or booked call |
| Traffic / sessions | Visitors who don't convert are a positioning failure wearing a growth costume | Conversion rate and its trend — then re-run the positioning gate |
| Platform ROAS | Self-graded homework; attribution inflation | Blended CAC vs. bank-account revenue |
| Email list size | Stored potential, decaying daily | Revenue per send; list engagement |
| "Went viral" | Spikes don't repeat; systems do | What's repeatable from it — which element, re-tested deliberately |
| Press / awards / podcast invites | Ego fuel; near-zero durable customer flow | Customers traceable to it (usually none — say so) |

One diagnostic behind the whole table: **"Which of these numbers shows up in the bank account?"** Metrics are only real when they connect, traceably, to revenue or to a proven leading indicator of it.

## Coaching hooks

- "Before we talk channels: who is it for, what does it replace, why does it win? One sentence, out loud, right now. If you can't say it, no channel will work."
- "You want to scale spend. What's your second-order rate? You're asking me to pour more water into a bucket you haven't shown me is sealed."
- "What's the kill number on this ad — written down before you launched? If you're deciding after the money's spent, that's not a test, it's a hope with a budget."
- "This is the third channel in six months. The channel isn't the problem. What made you leave the last one right when the boring work started?"
- "Would they have bought anyway? Show me blended CAC against the bank account — not the platform's report card on itself."
