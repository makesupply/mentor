# Discipline & Execution — Consistency Engineering and Learning Science

Read when the founder wants discipline, keeps breaking commitments, is designing routines or systems, or needs to learn a skill fast. Condensed from Report 9 (`references/research/report-9-discipline-learning.md`, 61 sources). Replication status stated throughout — this literature holds more corpses than any other in the skill (see the graveyard at the bottom, and `blocker-map.md` §8 for ego depletion's burial).

## 1. The avoidance reframe — discipline is architecture, not willpower

What survived the replication crisis: people high in trait self-control succeed by ENCOUNTERING fewer temptations, not resisting harder — habits, environment, pre-commitment [peer-reviewed, Hofmann; Galla & Duckworth meta-analysis]. The willpower-as-depletable-muscle model is dead (d ≈ 0.04 across two multisite preregistered replications; the glucose mechanism failed too). Coaching stance: when a founder confesses a discipline failure, the diagnostic question is never "why didn't you push through?" — it's "what did the environment put in front of you, and who designed the environment?" Remove decision points; don't armor them.

## 2. Habit engineering — the real numbers

- **The timeline** [peer-reviewed, Lally 2010 + 2024 meta-analysis of 20 studies]: median 66 days to automaticity, observed range 18–254; harder behaviors take longer (exercise ~91 days vs eating ~65). Tell founders "2–5 months, and that's normal" — the 21-day claim has no study behind it.
- **The single-miss rule** [peer-reviewed]: missing ONE day does not meaningfully damage the automaticity curve; only repeated multi-day misses (2+/week) do. A founder who missed Tuesday has lost nothing but Tuesday — say so, then get Wednesday.
- **Context is the trigger** [peer-reviewed, Wood — extensively replicated]: habits are cue–response associations, not motivation. Anchor every new routine to ONE fixed, unchanging cue (same desk, same time, same preceding action) and don't vary it during formation. Context changes (move, new job) break habits at any motivation level — flag transitions as habit-rebuild periods, not character tests.
- Habit stacking is [practitioner] — plausible via the cue literature, never isolated in trials. Use it, don't cite it as proven.

## 3. Goal mechanics — the lever and its guardrail

Specific, difficult goals beat vague ones at d = 0.52–0.82 [peer-reviewed, Locke & Latham — among the best-replicated findings in organizational psychology]; they work through attention, effort, persistence, and strategy search, moderated by feedback and self-efficacy. AND the same mechanism has documented side effects [peer-reviewed, "Goals Gone Wild"]: narrow focus that neglects everything unmeasured, distorted risk-taking, more unethical shortcuts, eroded intrinsic motivation. The guardrail is mandatory: **every specific/difficult goal gets an explicit "what this must not cost" boundary** — set at Intake for the mission number, restated at Decision Gate for big bets ("$1M by June — without touching the family-time boundary, product safety, or the price floor"). The two literatures aren't in conflict; they define the same lever's failure conditions.

## 4. If-then plans and their boundary conditions

The skill's backbone is the strongest survivor in this literature: implementation intentions at d = 0.65 across 94 studies, robust under bias correction and a 642-test update [peer-reviewed]. Extensions and caveats:
- **Coping planning** — pre-committing responses to specific anticipated obstacles — adds incremental value on top of plain if-thens [peer-reviewed] and is exactly what Foresight tripwires are (`foresight-protocols.md`).
- **WOOP/MCII is smaller than its reputation** (g ≈ 0.34 overall) and delivery-dependent: guided administration g = 0.47 vs self-administered worksheet g = 0.28 [peer-reviewed]. Administering it conversationally, one answer at a time, IS the guided mode — never assign it as homework.
- **Expectancy check before WOOP**: mental contrasting can backfire when the founder sees the obstacle as insurmountable [peer-reviewed, Oettingen's own boundary condition]. Low expectancy → build efficacy first (one small win, one reference case from the dossiers), then contrast.

## 5. Accountability — the real evidence, minus the folklore

- **The famous "65%/95% ASTD statistic" is fabricated.** ATD itself disclaims the study; no traceable source exists [debunked-but-popular]. This mentor never cites it.
- The real, traceable figure: Matthews (2007, N≈267) — written goals + action commitments + weekly progress reports to another person: 76% achieved vs 43% for goals-in-head [practitioner-adjacent: conference-presented, not independently replicated at scale — say the tier].
- What's mechanistically solid: felt obligation to a perceived evaluative audience reliably raises goal attainment [peer-reviewed]; AI-coaching studies show perceived accountability mediating goal progress. **The ledger is this skill's accountability engine — that is its evidence base.** Weekly written check-ins to a real audience (this mentor counts) beat private self-tracking.
- Commitment devices (deposit contracts, stickK-style) work but heterogeneously; strongest when loss-framed and automatically enforced [peer-reviewed]. Offer for high-stakes commitments the founder keeps breaking.

## 6. Environment design — the menu

- **Temptation bundling** [peer-reviewed, replicated at N≈6,800]: restrict a genuine "want" (favorite podcast, audiobook, specific coffee) EXCLUSIVELY to sessions of the avoided "should" (outreach block, bookkeeping). Original effect +51% gym visits; scale replication +10–14%, persisting ~17 weeks. Effects decay and break on disruption — rebuild the bundle after every vacation or crisis, don't mourn it.
- **Fresh-start effect** [peer-reviewed, Dai/Milkman/Riis]: temporal landmarks spike aspirational behavior, but ONLY when subjectively framed as a new period. Lever: frame any session, Monday, or month-start as the clean line — "the last six weeks are the old period; this is week one." Never wait for January.
- **Friction engineering**: distance and effort barriers on distractions, zero-friction staging for the target behavior (the deck open on the desktop before bed). Follows from the avoidance reframe (§1).

## 7. Tracking rules — objective or don't bother

Self-monitoring works at g = 0.32–0.40 ONLY with objective/automated tracking; subjective manual self-report tracking showed g = −0.02, statistically nothing [peer-reviewed meta-analysis]. Rules: metrics the founder tracks should come from systems (dashboard, bank feed, store analytics) into `state/metrics.md` — not from memory or vibes-journaling. Streak mercy rule (from §2): one miss never resets progress — an all-or-nothing streak tracker is working against the automaticity science. Gamification is boundary-conditioned [peer-reviewed, mixed]: narrative and social mechanics help; bare competitive leaderboards can LOWER intrinsic motivation and performance — don't gamify a founder who's already intrinsically driven.

## 8. Consistency vs intensity — the corrected story

- **Grit, corrected** [peer-reviewed, Credé meta-analysis, N=66,807; Duckworth acknowledged the correction]: r ≈ 0.18–0.20 with performance, largely redundant with conscientiousness. Real, modest, not the master key. Never sell grit as the answer; sell the architecture (§1–6) that makes persistence cheap.
- **Deliberate practice, corrected** [peer-reviewed, contested magnitude]: explains ~26% of performance variance in games, ~21% music — but ~4% in education and <1% in professions and among elite athletes [Macnamara meta-analyses; Ericsson disputed the method — live dispute, say so]. For business skills, practice volume alone is a weak predictor; what transfers is the STRUCTURE: real executed tasks + fast, specific feedback loops beat unstructured repetition [peer-reviewed direction]. A founder grinding 80-hour weeks isn't buying proportional skill — the feedback loop is the multiplier.

## 9. Learning protocol for the self-taught founder

Dunlosky's utility ratings [peer-reviewed, the definitive review]: HIGH utility — retrieval practice (self-testing) and spaced review. MODERATE — self-explanation, interleaving. LOW — highlighting, rereading, summarizing: the most-used and least effective techniques. The founder rereading business books and highlighting is doing comfortable, low-yield work.
Administer the protocol: (1) after any course/book/analysis, the mentor quizzes the founder from memory — retrieval, not recognition; (2) spaced re-checks at roughly days 1, 3, 7, 14 (fold into Weekly Review); (3) every insight converts to an if-then within the session — knowledge without a cue-triggered plan is the knowing-doing gap in action [peer-reviewed direction]; (4) skills learn fastest against real executed work with fast feedback (§8) — ship the cold email, review the replies, adjust, repeat.

## The graveyard — never say these

| Popular claim | Truth |
|---|---|
| "21 days to form a habit" | No study basis; median 66 days, range 18–254 |
| "Willpower depletes like a muscle" / "protect your willpower reserves" | d ≈ 0.04 in two multisite replications — dead |
| "Sugar restores willpower" | Failed replication; weak evidential value |
| "65% commitment / 95% accountability appointment (ASTD)" | Fabricated; ATD disclaims it |
| "Breaking a streak resets your progress" | One miss doesn't bend the curve |
| "10,000 hours is why elites are elite" | <1% of variance among elites; ~4% in professions |
| "Grit is the #1 predictor of success" | r ≈ 0.18–0.20, redundant with conscientiousness |
| "Journaling your habits changes them" | Subjective self-tracking: g ≈ −0.02; only objective tracking works |
| "Leaderboards and badges boost motivation" | Can lower intrinsic motivation and performance |

## Coaching hooks

- "You didn't fail a willpower test — nobody passes those. Who designed the environment where that temptation was one click away? Redesign it today."
- "It's been three weeks and it's not automatic yet — right on schedule. The real median is 66 days. You missed Tuesday? The curve doesn't care. Get Wednesday."
- "That goal needs its guardrail before I'll log it: what is this number NOT allowed to cost you?"
- "You want an accountability trick — the famous statistic everyone quotes is fabricated. Here's what's real: written goal, written plan, weekly report to someone who reads it. You're doing that right now. That's the whole trick."
- "You've read four books on sales this quarter. Close the book. I'm going to quiz you on the last one, and then the ONE idea you keep becomes an if-then with a date."
- "Eighty-hour weeks buy you almost no skill without a feedback loop — practice explains under 5% in professions. What did you SHIP this week, and who told you specifically what was wrong with it?"
