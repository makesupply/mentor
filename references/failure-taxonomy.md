# Failure Taxonomy — Why Businesses Die, Stall, and Recover

Read for Hard Truth audits, for the Foresight mode's scenario library, and whenever the founder quotes failure folklore or shows an early-warning signal. Condensed from Report 6 (`references/research/report-6-failure-taxonomy.md`). Evidence tiers matter here more than anywhere — say them out loud.

## Base rates — the anti-folklore weapon

The "90% fail in year one" claim has never appeared in any federal dataset. The real curve [government data, BLS census-level, 1994–2024 cohorts]:

| Milestone | Survival (all US industries) |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | ~80% |
| Year 5 | ~49–51% |
| Year 10 | ~34% |

- Industry matters: 5-year survival spans ~40% (information, mining) to ~55–66% (healthcare, agriculture) [government data]. Restaurants: only 17% fail in year one — LOWER than the 19% all-services average; median lifespan ~4.5 years [peer-reviewed, 1.9M records].
- International: 5-year survival ~34% (Korea) to ~63% (Sweden); US near the OECD average at ~50% [government data].
- VC-backed startups fail MORE, not less: ~70% shut down or fail to return capital within their funding lifecycle [practitioner survey, definitions vary — say so].

How the mentor uses this: when the founder is catastrophizing ("everyone fails"), give the real base rate. When they're invincible ("we're different"), give the same number from the other side. Half of businesses alive at year five is neither doom nor safety — it's a planning input.

## Why they actually die — two evidence layers, one correction

**Academic layer [peer-reviewed, systematic reviews of 41–95 studies]:** four interacting domains — financial, managerial/organizational, product-market, legal/environmental. The most frequently cited domain is MANAGERIAL (35 of 41 reviewed articles): inexperience, poor strategy, weak team — ahead of money and market.

**Practitioner layer [self-reported post-mortems — apply the bias correction]:**
| Cause | % citing it | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ran out of cash | up to 70% (CB Insights 2026, N=431) | The final SYMPTOM, not the root — CB Insights says so itself |
| No market need / no PMF | 42–43% | Most-cited root cause across vintages |
| Bad timing / macro | 29% | |
| Team problems | 20–23% | |
| Outcompeted | 19–20% | |
| Unit economics / pricing | 15–19% | |
| Lack of focus | 13% | |

**The attribution-bias correction [peer-reviewed]:** failed founders systematically externalize — they over-report financing/market/timing and under-report their own managerial and strategic errors. The academic layer (managerial causes dominate) is what the self-reports hide. Application: when a founder autopsy blames the market, the funding climate, or the platform, probe the managerial layer before accepting it. Gently, once, with evidence — then move forward (feedforward rules still apply).

Cofounder conflict contributes to roughly 65% of startup failures per Wasserman's Founder's Dilemmas research [practitioner/academic-adjacent — the report's own tiering was inconsistent on this one; treat as strong-directional, not census-grade].

## Stall vs. fail — the plateau mechanism

Stalling is a different animal from dying, and the mentor should name which one is happening.

- **Greiner's growth-stage model** (creativity→direction→delegation→coordination→collaboration, each ending in a named crisis) is a useful DIAGNOSTIC HEURISTIC, not a validated law: large cross-national tests found no general support; smaller single-country studies found partial-to-substantial support [peer-reviewed, honestly mixed]. Use its vocabulary ("this smells like a leadership crisis — the informal founder-centric mode is breaking"), never its stages as destiny.
- **Documented plateau drivers** [peer-reviewed, recurring across reviews]: founder skill/versatility ceilings, inability to delegate, and threat rigidity — doubling down on the familiar strategy under stress. These stall firms; they don't necessarily kill them.
- **What breaks plateaus** (thinner literature, say so): deliberate delegation and professionalization of management (`hiring-and-delegation.md`, doctrine Law 11), plus boundary-setting that prevents burnout-driven decision decay.
- The quantified "$1M→$10M valley of death" base rate does not exist in the literature [SOURCE MISSING] — if the founder asks "what % make the jump," the honest answer is that nobody has measured it to census standard; the mechanism (informal coordination breaking before systems replace it) is what's documented.

## Early-warning indicators — the checkable list

Financial prediction models decay fast with horizon: ~52% accuracy one year before bankruptcy, ~11% at four years [peer-reviewed] — so early warning lives in TRENDS and non-financial signals, not a one-time score.

| Signal | Evidence | The mentor's check |
|---|---|---|
| Runway trend (cash ÷ burn, in weeks) | Cash-out is the universal final symptom | Weekly cash ritual (`finance-fundamentals.md`) — is the number shrinking two periods in a row? |
| Altman Z″ drifting toward distress (<1.81) | [peer-reviewed], 1-year horizon only | For asset-carrying businesses at scale; directional otherwise |
| Management churn / shortening employee tenure | Omega Score: improves default prediction beyond financials [peer-reviewed] | "Who left in the last two quarters, and why?" |
| Losing or wobbling a major client | 26% of struggling founders reported it pre-failure [practitioner survey] | Concentration check (`b2b-playbook.md` axis 3) |
| Growth stalling vs plan | 42% reported "not growing fast enough" pre-failure [practitioner survey] | Stage-gate deltas in `state/metrics.md` |
| Founder boundary erosion | Boundary-holders: 6% high burnout vs 67% without [practitioner survey, N=300+] | The Weekly Review boundary question is a leading indicator, not politeness |
| Repeated commitment breaks | This skill's own ledger | 3+ consecutive broken/absent commitments = the escalation rule fires |

## Recovery — what's actually documented

Honest state: rigorous quantified turnaround data (matched pairs, before/after) does not exist at BLS-grade rigor [SOURCE MISSING] — this is low-confidence territory; never invent a recovery statistic. What IS documented:

- **The boundary lever**: founders who set and held work-life boundaries showed low-burnout rates of 45% vs 6%... inverted: high burnout 67% → 45% band shift with one behavior change [practitioner survey, Lehigh/Nasdaq N=300+]. The single clearest documented recovery behavior.
- **Pivot triggers**: negative customer reaction and a flawed business model are the most common triggers of failure-avoiding pivots [peer-reviewed pivot-trigger study]; 40% of surveyed founders pivoted specifically to avoid failure in one year [practitioner survey].
- Everything else in "turnaround" content is anecdote. Use the crisis protocol (`crisis-protocols.md`) and the documented founder cases (`founder-dossiers.md`, `b2b-playbook.md` — ConvertKit, Baremetrics, True Classic) as illustrations, labeled as survivor stories, never as base rates.

## Coaching hooks

- "You said 90% of businesses fail in year one. No federal dataset has ever shown that — the real number is about 20%. Your fear is real; your statistic isn't. Let's plan against the real curve."
- "Your autopsy blames the market. The research says failed founders systematically blame outside causes while the most-cited real driver is managerial. If a cold-eyed operator reviewed your last two quarters, what decision would THEY name?"
- "Cash didn't kill it — cash is how it died. What was the root: no market need, pricing, or a call you made? Name one."
- "You're not dying, you're stalling — and stalls have a known mechanism: the informal founder-does-everything mode breaks before systems replace it. Which function breaks first if you double revenue tomorrow?"
- "Who's left the company in the last six months? Management churn and shrinking tenure predict failure better than your P&L does."
