# Hiring & Delegation — Building the Machine

Read when the founder is drowning in low-leverage work, asking "do I need to hire," managing badly, or avoiding a firing. Companion to doctrine.md Law 11: the founder's job migrates from doing the work to building the machine that does the work.

## The delegation ladder (administer, don't describe)

Law 11 operationalized. Every task climbs four rungs: **Do it → Document it → Delegate it → Audit it.** Skipping a rung is the failure mode — delegating undocumented work produces rework and "it's faster if I do it myself," which the founder then cites as proof delegation doesn't work.

Run the ladder on ONE real task, live in session:

1. **Pick.** Make the founder name one repeatable task they did this week that someone or something else could do. Reject strategic tasks and one-offs — it must recur.
2. **Document.** Before the session ends or as a dated commitment: a checklist or 5-minute screen recording capturing how they actually do it, including the judgment calls. If they can't document it, it isn't ready to delegate — it's still at rung 1 and that's the real finding.
3. **Delegate.** Assign to a rung from the leverage menu below, with a definition of done and a deadline. One owner. Not "help me with" — "you own."
4. **Audit.** Founder reviews output on a declared cadence (daily → weekly → monthly as trust earns down), against the doc, not against vibes. Auditing is a permanent founder job; abdication is not delegation.

Commit the whole thing as an if-then (unblocking-protocols.md, If-Then Builder). One task per week through the ladder compounds; ten tasks at once stalls.

## Low-leverage hours audit

Before any hiring conversation: one week of honest time accounting. No tool required — a note per half-day is enough.

1. Founder logs what they actually did for 5 working days, in blocks.
2. Tag each block: **$-value class** (would a $15/hr, $50/hr, or only-the-founder person do this?) and **repeatable? (Y/N)**.
3. The delegation candidates are every block tagged low-$ AND repeatable. Total the hours. Say the number back to them out loud — "you spent 14 hours being your own $15/hr employee" — before discussing any hire.
4. Anything repeatable and documented (or documentable) goes on the delegation backlog, ordered by hours reclaimed.

If the founder resists the audit, that's a block, not a scheduling issue — route to unblocking-protocols.md (team/hire/delegation avoidance row).

## The leverage menu

Modern founders should exhaust AI and contractor leverage before headcount [inference — Native's 8-person team at $25–35M is the pre-AI proof of how far founder+systems stretches; AI lowers that floor further]. Employees are the most expensive, slowest-to-reverse instrument on the menu. Match the instrument to the task, not to the founder's loneliness.

| Instrument | Cost shape | Speed to start | Mgmt burden | Fits when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI agents/tools | Lowest, scales ~free | Hours | Prompting + auditing outputs | Repeatable, documented, digital, tolerable error rate |
| Contractor/freelancer | Per-project or hourly, no benefits | Days | Scoping + QA per deliverable | Defined deliverables, spiky workload, specialist skill |
| Part-time specialist | Retainer, fraction of salary | 1–2 weeks | Weekly check-in | Recurring function needing judgment, not yet full-time volume |
| Employee | Highest: salary + taxes + management + severance risk | Weeks–months | Continuous: 1:1s, feedback, growth | Core recurring function, full-time volume proven by the audit, judgment + context accumulation matter |

Rule of administration: when the founder says "I need to hire someone," make them name the specific tasks first (from the audit), then walk the menu top-down. Only what survives AI and contractor scrutiny justifies headcount.

## First-hire sequencing

Hire against the founder's **bottleneck function** — the thing constraining revenue that only they currently do — not against their **discomfort** — the thing they hate doing. Founders reliably hire to escape discomfort (usually admin/CX) while staying the bottleneck [practitioner pattern; frameworks conflict on the "right" first hire — see stage-playbooks.md Stage 1 team note]. The audit reveals discomfort; the stage gates reveal the bottleneck.

For the researched DTC vertical, defer to stage-playbooks.md team notes per stage (Stage 1: founder runs acquisition personally, headcount can be zero with an AI workforce; Stage 2: ops lead + retention specialist as founder exits ad buying; Stage 3: leadership layer). For any other business, generalize the principle: identify which function gates the next stage, delegate everything else down the leverage menu, and hire only where accumulated context and judgment are the job.

## Hiring mechanics for first-time managers

- **Scorecard before posting** [practitioner — Smart & Street, *Who*]: write the outcomes the role must deliver in 12 months (3–5, measurable) and the deal-breaker behaviors BEFORE writing the job ad. If the founder can't write the scorecard, they don't understand the role well enough to hire it — back to rung 2 of the ladder.
- **Work sample beats interview polish** [peer-reviewed — selection-research meta-analyses in the Schmidt & Hunter tradition consistently rank work-sample tests and structured interviews far above unstructured interviews as predictors of job performance; exact coefficients are contested across revisions — verify live if a decision hangs on the number]. Administer: design one paid, time-boxed sample of the actual job (real task, 2–8 hours) and score it against the scorecard before any culture chat. Charisma is the noise; the sample is the signal.
- **Structured over improvised**: same questions, same order, every candidate, scored against the scorecard. The founder's "gut read" is the unstructured interview — the worst instrument in the research wearing the most confidence.
- **Hire slow.** A bad first hire costs cash, months, and the founder's belief in delegation itself. An empty seat is cheaper than the wrong person in it.

## Managing without experience

Three mechanisms cover most of first-time management. Install them; don't improvise.

1. **Weekly 1:1, non-negotiable, Campbell five-topic structure** (unblocking-protocols.md, Campbell Kit): Personal / Peer relationships / Performance with metrics / Management-leadership / Innovation. 30 minutes. Canceling 1:1s is the first symptom of the founder sliding back down the ladder.
2. **Single owner per outcome.** Every metric, project, and recurring task has exactly one name on it. Two owners is zero owners. The founder's name should be disappearing from the list over time — that IS the job migration.
3. **Feedback within 48 hours.** Praise or correction, delivered specific and private, within two days of the observation. Stored-up feedback becomes a performance review ambush and teaches the team nothing in between. For correction, use feedforward format (unblocking-protocols.md): one behavior, two next-time actions, no autopsy.

If a report is underperforming, work the team, then the problem (Campbell): is the doc bad, the scope ambiguous, the owner overloaded — or the person wrong for the seat? Only the last one is a firing conversation.

## Firing fast

Founders know months before they act — the pattern is near-universal [practitioner consensus; no founder-specific peer-reviewed timeline — say so if pressed]. While they wait, the cost is borne by the best people still there: A-players carry the load, watch tolerated mediocrity, and quietly conclude standards are negotiable. The kind-feeling delay is the cruelest option in the room.

Administer the keeper test [practitioner — Netflix, Hastings/McCord]: **"If this person told you tomorrow they were leaving for a similar job elsewhere, would you fight to keep them?"** Make the founder answer yes or no out loud, no hedging. A "no" plus more than one honest improvement cycle already given = the decision is made; what remains is scheduling and decency. Then:

1. One clean conversation — decision, not debate. Specifics ready, blame absent, severance fair.
2. Same-day, honest, brief communication to the team. Silence breeds a worse story than the truth.
3. Autopsy the HIRE, not the person: which scorecard line was wrong or missing? Feed it back into the next hiring loop.

If the founder keeps agreeing and not acting, that's decision paralysis — route to fear-setting (unblocking-protocols.md), and make page 3 (cost of inaction) about the team, not the founder.

## Coaching hooks

- "List everything you did this week that a $15-an-hour person or an AI agent could do. Now tell me again that you can't afford leverage."
- "Is this a hiring problem or a trust problem? Show me the document you'd hand the new person. No document — it's neither; the work isn't delegable yet, and that's on you."
- "You want to hire for the work you hate. What's the function actually capping revenue right now? That's the hire. The rest goes on the menu."
- "Keeper test: they resign tomorrow — do you fight to keep them? You paused. Who on your team is paying for that pause?"
- "You don't need a person, you need a system with a name on it. Whose name — and when do you audit it?"
