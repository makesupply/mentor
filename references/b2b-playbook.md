# B2B Playbook — Selling to Businesses

Read when the founder's business model is B2B — SaaS, agency/productized services, consulting, wholesale/distribution/manufacturing (tagged in `state/founder-context.md`). Condensed from Report 4 and Report 4b (`references/research/report-4-b2b-founder-case-studies.md`, `report-4b-b2b-gap-followup.md`; 131 sources combined). This module translates the doctrine to B2B structure; `doctrine.md` still applies where mechanisms match.

## First move: diagnose the structure, not the label

The reports' core finding: the biggest determinant of which playbook applies is NOT "B2B vs DTC" — it is the founder's specific combination of four axes. Make the founder answer all four before applying any case lesson:

| Axis | The question | What the evidence says |
|---|---|---|
| Sales-cycle length | "What's your ACV, and how many people say yes?" | Cycle scales with deal size, not B2B-ness: <$15K ACV closes in 14–30 days; $15–100K ~60 days; >$100K ~135 days; >$250K runs 9–18 months. Buying committees grew from ~5.4 stakeholders (2015) to ~11 (2025) [documented]. |
| Cash timing | "How much revenue is sitting in unpaid invoices?" | B2B inverts DTC: receivables dominate (wholesale terms 30–75 days; custom B2B 45–120 days). Profitable-on-paper-but-cash-starved is a B2B-specific death with no DTC analog [documented]. |
| Concentration risk | "What % of revenue is your biggest account?" | B2B concentration is account-based (P1 Industries deliberately engineered out of GE-anchor dependence); DTC's is channel-based. Different axis, same kill mechanism [founder-claimed]. |
| Margin structure & delivery labor | "What's your gross margin after the human labor — the real one, not the deck's?" | Pricing narrative must match delivery economics. Bench priced like SaaS (implying 70–80% GM) while delivering labor-heavy bookkeeping (actual 20–40%) — $113M raised, bankrupt Dec 2024 [documented]. |

Plus capital intensity: software and services bootstrap; factories can't. Isembard raised $50M deliberately because facilities can't be financed from thin operating margins — the opposite choice from the bootstrapped SaaS cohort, and equally rational [founder-claimed].

## The B2B patterns

**B1 — Pricing is the most-repeated lever, and the error is always underpricing.** The finelink.io postmortem names underpricing "strategic self-sabotage" — early adopters in genuine pain pay premiums that fund everything else [founder-claimed]. DesignJoy grandfathered 2017 clients at $400/mo while new clients paid ~10x, creating an "unsustainable workload" he had to un-grandfather his way out of [founder-claimed]. Martell, from coaching hundreds of SaaS companies: "90% of the time pricing is off in that company" [founder-claimed]. Application: when a B2B founder brings any growth problem, check pricing first.

**B2 — Prove the motion before hiring the function.** Ahrefs hired no marketer until ~$7M ARR [secondhand]; Atlassian hired its first commissioned salesperson in 2014, past $100M revenue [documented]; ConvertKit's Barry personally emailed bloggers one at a time before building any repeatable system [founder-claimed]. Hire specialists to SCALE a proven motion, never to discover one. And budget reality into the first sales hire: 50–70% of first sales hires fail, average ramp is ~5.7 months, and "your first sales hire will close fewer deals than you did" is the expected outcome, not the exception [secondhand, recruiting-industry analysis]. Pigford's founder-reported version: a new hire may not show positive return for 12–18 months [founder-claimed].

**B3 — Which function the founder keeps depends on the business type.** Product businesses delegate sales LAST (Atlassian, Ahrefs — the product sells while the founder builds). Service businesses invert this: delivery is delegated FIRST and the founder keeps sales/relationships longest — Huberman closed Red Bull himself while a small team executed from month one [founder-claimed]; Siu kept client relationships and outsourced delivery entirely via referral partners for a 25–30% lifetime cut before hiring any delivery capacity [founder-claimed]. Diagnose which side the founder's business sits on before advising who to hire.

**B4 — Self-serve vs founder-led sales is a structural choice with a stated price.** Atlassian's CRO: "Atlassian never did 200% growth years. Atlassian just did like 35% growth for 20 years... you are going to grow your business more slowly... but more profitably" [primary quote]. Self-serve works when the product can be trialed instantly online; it does not transfer to relationship/trust-bought categories (P1's precision machining, complex consulting) [founder-claimed].

**B5 — Founder-led sales is unscalable on purpose, and the real numbers are rarer than the internet claims.** Real, named data points: Barry's concierge phase ~100 cold emails → 5–7 customers (5–7% close, doing the migration FOR them) [founder-claimed]; Single Grain's discovery-call-to-close rate 15–20% [documented case study]; Efti's operational cold-call reach rate 10–15% of dials connecting to a decision-maker, with <10% meaning fix targeting before anything else [founder-claimed, vendor-adjacent]; Zapier's $100 beta charge as an intent filter [founder-claimed]. BENCHMARK GUARD: vendor cold-email "benchmarks" (1–9% reply claims) are marketing; the largest transparent dataset (Belkins, 7.5M sends) shows ~0.45% reply rates, and the popular claim that "founder outreach converts 3x better than reps" has NO study behind it — a source that went looking said so explicitly [secondhand]. The mentor never quotes a vendor outreach benchmark as a target; anchor on the founder's own funnel data instead.

**B6 — Scale beyond yourself by institutionalizing judgment.** Bower's McKinsey is the canonical case: codified principles (1937), up-or-out promotion, hiring criteria, CEO-only engagement — converting one man's credibility into a firm that grew billings from $2M (1950) to $200M+ (1967) and outlived every founder [documented]. The modern small-firm version: fractional-CFO firms like Burkland publish tiered fixed-retainer catalogs ($1,600/$2,500/$4,200/mo with named deliverables) so junior staff deliver defined scope while senior CFOs spread across accounts [documented pricing pages]. A practitioner analysis claims ~80% of consultancies plateau at £2–5M because the operating model never runs without the founder [secondhand, unverified] — directionally consistent with every case here. Transfers to any judgment-based service business; transfers poorly to product businesses where the constraint is capital or code.

**B7 — Anchor client, then diversify — and watch the champion.** P1 used GE as the anchor to get credibility, then deliberately broke out of single-customer concentration [founder-claimed]. Champion turnover — the internal advocate leaving the buying organization — is a uniquely B2B churn risk with no DTC analog.

**B8 — Cash discipline translated: DSO is the B2B enemy, and financing it has a known ladder.** Doctrine Law 6's cash discipline runs through receivables here, not inventory. The documented graduation path: spot factoring (year 0–1) → whole-ledger factoring ($1–10M revenue) → asset-based lending revolver ($5–50M, ~200–400bps cheaper) → bank line (usually $20M+ with 2+ profitable years) [secondhand, lending-industry aggregation]. Factoring costs: 70–96% advance rates, 1–4% per 30 days outstanding (≈12–48% APR-equivalent) [documented cost structures, vendor-adjacent]. Named cases: Best Broadcast doubled sales $1.4M→$4M+ within 18 months of factoring its 45–90-day receivables [secondhand, vendor-published but named]; Kosher Depot entered a two-year $3.3M venture-merchant deal advancing cash against customer invoices to fund inventory when suppliers demanded cash-in-advance [documented, CFO.com trade journalism, 2005]. PO financing (lender pays the supplier against a confirmed order) is the standard bridge for import/distribution fulfilling orders beyond working capital [documented].

**B9 — Leverage kills faster than slow growth.** Thrasio: $3.5B raised, 200+ brands acquired at peak-2021 multiples, ~$2–3M/day burn by 2022–23, one $450M inventory mistake the co-founder calls the moment "the dream was over," Chapter 11 in Feb 2024 [documented/founder-claimed]. Proving unit economics does NOT make acquisition velocity safe — integration capacity is its own constraint, and debt turns a multiples reset into bankruptcy. The Law 7 inventory lesson at 1000x scale.

**B10 — Match the pricing narrative to the delivery economics.** Bench Accounting is the inverse of B1: not underpricing, but pricing a labor-heavy service as if it were high-margin software. $113M raised, ~650 employees, abrupt shutdown Dec 27 2024, $65M+ debts in bankruptcy — root cause independently diagnosed as the structural margin mismatch, papered over with automation that "didn't work properly" per former staff [documented, TechCrunch]. When a founder says "it's basically SaaS," ask what percentage of delivery is human hours.

**B11 — The failure pattern: cash-out is the symptom, not the cause.** Across the failure cohort (Corq, Juttu, finelink.io, Tutorspree, Bench, Thrasio), root causes were poor demand validation before building, mispriced offers or margin mismatch, single-channel dependency, leverage outrunning integration, and bootstrapping past the point where founder capacity could cover critical functions [founder-claimed postmortems + documented bankruptcies]. Juttu set an explicit survival bar (10 paying clients by month 5), hit 2, and shut down honestly — a pre-written kill gate working as designed (`validation-playbook.md`).

## Condensed dossiers

**ConvertKit/Kit (Nathan Barry)** — the best-documented near-death-and-recovery case: launched 2013, flatlined/declined 22 months, bottomed at $1,207 MRR Oct 2014 (advisor said shut it down). Barry injected $50K personal savings, killed his profitable ~$250K/yr ebook business, and did concierge migrations by hand → $97K MRR by Dec 2015 (73x in ~14 months), $1M MRR 2018, $43M ARR / 99.5%+ NDR by Dec 2024 [founder-claimed; public Baremetrics dashboard for the bottom]. Only outside capital ever: ~$1.8M from angels.

**Atlassian** — $100M+ revenue with zero commissioned salespeople (first sales hire 2014, 12 years in); bootstrapped 8 years, then $60M growth equity AFTER profitability; $5.2B FY2025 [documented]. Farquhar: "We are not anti-salespeople; we are pro-automation."

**Ahrefs (Dmytro Gerasymenko)** — $300K personal savings → ~$100M ARR with no sales team and first marketing hire at ~$7M; charged $7 for trials to filter intent; company-disclosed ~$257M revenue across 2020–2022 [documented via engineering blog]. Cleanest pure bootstrap in the cohort — never even angel money.

**Mailchimp** — bootstrapped 20 years from severance pay → $12B Intuit acquisition (2021), the largest bootstrapped exit on record [documented]; 2009 freemium launch "tripled revenue overnight" [founder-claimed].

**Basecamp/37signals** — $30K founder money; rejected 100+ VC offers (one minority Bezos investment); deliberately capped headcount for two decades; flat $99/mo pricing was ALSO a documented monetization mistake on large accounts — even doctrine cases carry scars [documented].

**Zapier (Wade Foster)** — $100 beta charge as an intent filter; found customers in competitors' help forums; $50M ARR by 2018 on a $1.3M seed "operated like a bootstrap" [founder-claimed].

**Hawke Media (Erik Huberman)** — agency, zero outside funding: ~$900K (2014) → $5.5M (2016) [documented, LABJ] → $621M gross including pass-through ad spend by 2026 [founder-claimed — gross, not fee revenue; he states the distinction]. Delegation-of-delivery from day one ("I'm your outsourced CMO, and I've got a team behind me"); founder kept sales. Disclosed operating framework: 30% growth / 35% servicing / 15% overhead / 20% EBITDA target (runs 22–23%) [founder-claimed]. 23 agency acquisitions funded from operating cash flow — the anti-Thrasio.

**Single Grain (Eric Siu)** — bought a failing agency for $2 while it lost $27K/month on $1.5M revenue; +$30K/month profit within 8 months [founder-claimed]. Kept client relationships, outsourced delivery via 25–30% lifetime referral deals (~$250–300K/yr pure margin) before hiring delivery [founder-claimed]. Documented 15–20% discovery-to-close rate; owned-media moat (Marketing School, ~80M downloads) [documented/founder-claimed; current revenue estimates conflict $9.7M–$14M — treat as trajectory].

**Baremetrics (Josh Pigford)** — built in 8 days, first paying customers day one from a single tweet, $1K MRR in month one, ~30% MoM for 4–6 months, first hire at ~$10K MRR (inbound from public building) [founder-claimed]. Near-death with numbers: a part-time CFO found 6–8 weeks of runway; 15% team pay cut + 30% founder cut; team stayed [founder-claimed, primary interview].

**Dan Martell / SaaS Academy** — productized coaching: publishes ~90% of frameworks free as the trust funnel; ~3,000 founders through the program at reported ~$25K/yr [secondhand; the "$30M+ revenue" claim is unverified]. His pricing lever: a setup fee that cut CAC payback under 60 days [founder-claimed].

**Marvin Bower / McKinsey** — bought an 18-person firm in 1939 after the founder's death; codified principles, up-or-out, elite hiring; billings $2M→$200M+ (1950–1967); sold his equity back at book value in 1963 to cement institutional stewardship [documented].

**Bench Accounting** — the margin-mismatch failure: founded 2012, $113M VC, ~650 employees; priced as SaaS (implying 70–80% GM), delivered as labor (actual 20–40%); bank called the venture debt; abrupt shutdown Dec 27 2024, $65M+ debts, founder had been replaced by the board pre-collapse [documented, TechCrunch/Wikipedia].

**Thrasio** — the leverage failure: 2018 wine-cellar start → $500M+ revenue and ~$10B valuation by 2021 on $3.5B raised; $2–3M/day burn, a $450M inventory mistake, Chapter 11 Feb 2024 [documented/founder-claimed]. Fast is not safe.

**P1 Industries** — bought a struggling machine shop (~2004), one used lathe → 150 people / ~$50M serving power, defense, aerospace [founder-claimed]. Named constraints: GE concentration (engineered out) and workforce development — in manufacturing, talent is the bottleneck, not demand.

**Freshway Foods** — one truck (1988) → ~$130M produce wholesaler [founder-claimed; interim years SOURCE MISSING]; vertically integrated its own logistics subsidiary rather than depend on third-party freight.

**Isembard** — the deliberate contrast: raised $50M Series A because factory networks cannot be bootstrapped; capital intensity legitimately overrides bootstrap doctrine [founder-claimed].

## Coaching hooks

- "How much of your revenue is sitting in unpaid invoices right now? Profitable on paper and cash-starved is how B2B businesses actually die — what's your DSO?"
- "What percent of revenue is your biggest account? Say the number out loud. P1 Industries treated that number as an emergency and engineered out of it."
- "You want to hire a salesperson. Show me the founder-led motion that's already closing — and know the base rate: half to two-thirds of first sales hires fail, and yours will close less than you do for months. You hire to scale a proven motion, not to discover one."
- "You run a service business and you're the one delivering. Huberman handed delivery to the team in month one and kept the selling. Which half are you refusing to let go of — and is it the right half?"
- "What's your gross margin after the human hours — the real one, not the pitch deck's? Bench raised $113M pricing labor like software. It died at Christmas with clients locked out of their books."
- "Did you set that price from your costs or from their pain? The finelink postmortem calls what you're doing strategic self-sabotage — the customers in real pain would have paid more."
- "Barry closed 5–7 of every 100 cold emails by doing the migration FOR them. What's your concierge version — the unscalable thing you're too proud to do?"
