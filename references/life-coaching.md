# Life Coaching — The Founder as a Whole Person

Read in Life mode, and whenever the Personal check in Strategy Session or the boundary check in Weekly Review surfaces something real. Condensed from Report 7 (`references/research/report-7-whole-person-coaching.md`, 64 sources). Explicitly NOT therapy — evidence-tiered coaching with the referral triggers armed (see §8 and `blocker-map.md`).

## 1. What coaching can actually do — calibrate the promise

Coaching works at moderate effect: goal-directed self-regulation g = 0.74, performance g = 0.60, well-being g = 0.46 [peer-reviewed meta-analysis, Theeboom 2014] — but controlled designs show roughly a third of the uncontrolled headline (g = 0.39 vs 1.15), and MORE SESSIONS ARE NOT RELIABLY BETTER. Rapport matters most EARLY (working alliance r = .41 overall, but the effect is front-loaded and explained only ~2% of follow-up goal attainment in one trial [contested]). Design implication the mentor should own out loud when useful: a 2026 preregistered study found AI-run values/goal work improves progress via PERCEIVED ACCOUNTABILITY, not by changing motivational structure [single study]. The ledger IS the mechanism. Don't pretend to be deeper than the evidence; be relentlessly consistent instead.

## 2. The MI toolkit — technique, not vibes

Process-level meta-analysis: MI-consistent TECHNIQUES predict client change talk (r = .55) and outcomes; therapist empathy and "MI spirit" paths were NOT supported [peer-reviewed]. Overall MI effects are small (g ≈ 0.22; near zero against active treatments) — it's a style upgrade, not a magic lever. Ranked toolkit for every Life session:
1. **Reflections** — paraphrase what the founder said before adding anything; reinforce their own change-talk when it appears.
2. **Affirmations** — specific, earned (voice rules already require this).
3. **Open questions that elicit THEIR reasons** — "why does this matter to you?" before any tactic.
4. **Never persuade.** Persuasion is the weakest and sometimes counterproductive technique [peer-reviewed]. The founder argues themselves into change; the mentor supplies the questions and the evidence.

## 3. Values and self-concordance — whose goal is this?

Self-concordance is one of the most secure constructs available [peer-reviewed, replicated 30+ longitudinal studies, 4 cultures]: goals pursued for autonomous reasons (intrinsic interest, identified values) get more sustained effort and higher attainment — and ONLY autonomous goal attainment produces well-being gains. Administer the check on any big goal, including the revenue number in founder-context:
- "Rate this goal: are you doing it because it's genuinely yours (interest/values), or because you'd feel guilty not to, or because someone else expects it?" (intrinsic / identified vs introjected / external)
- An introjected goal ("should," guilt, imagined audience) predicts grind-without-satisfaction — flag it, then either re-anchor the goal to a value the founder actually holds or renegotiate the goal itself at Intake-level.
Values-clarification methods measurably reduce values-incongruent choices and decisional regret [peer-reviewed meta-analysis — from medical decision-aid research; business transfer is inference, say so]. The "Wheel of Life" has NO located psychometric validation [SOURCE MISSING] — use domain check-ins as structured conversation (energy/health, partner/family, friends/peers, meaning outside the business, money feelings, play/recovery), never as a scored assessment.

## 4. Relationships and the partner

Evidence is mixed and specific — don't sermonize:
- One spouse's entrepreneurial entry lowers the other's well-being through two named channels: LONGER HOURS and REDUCED INCOME; solo start-ups transmit strain mainly via income, employer start-ups via time [peer-reviewed, 628-couple UK panel]. So the coachable variables are concrete: hours boundaries and income floor — not "communicate more."
- Couples who co-found show no relationship cost and real financial upside [peer-reviewed, Danish registry].
- GOAL CONGRUITY between partners — not what the goals are, but whether they're shared — was the strongest predictor of spousal satisfaction [single study].
- The circulating "entrepreneurs are 15% more likely to divorce" stat could not be traced to a verifiable study [SOURCE MISSING] — never quote it.
Coaching moves: make the hours/income channels explicit ("which one is your household absorbing right now?"); prescribe the goal-congruity conversation as a commitment ("walk your partner through the goal and the timeline; ask what it costs them; report back").

## 5. Loneliness — reframe beats "network more"

The best-evidenced domain in this file. Interventions targeting MALADAPTIVE SOCIAL COGNITION (the founder's beliefs about being unsupported, being a burden, needing to have it together) outperform social-support and social-activity interventions by 2–4x [peer-reviewed meta-analyses: cognitive ES up to −1.58 vs −0.34/−0.27; 122-RCT meta SMD ≈ −0.50]. Founder application: when isolation shows up, don't just prescribe a mastermind — challenge the belief first ("what do you assume happens if you tell another founder you're struggling?"), THEN prescribe the contact. Peer support is also the documented mitigator of impostor-driven performance damage (`blocker-map.md` §3).

## 6. Money psychology

- **Money scripts** [peer-reviewed, validated KMSI-R]: four patterns — avoidance ("money is bad / I don't deserve it"), worship ("more money fixes everything"), status ("net worth = self-worth"), vigilance (frugal, anxious, secretive). Avoidance and worship predict lower net worth and more revolving credit; vigilance predicts wealth plus anxiety. Administer conversationally against a REAL decision: "Finish the sentence: money is… / People with money are… / If I raise my prices, that makes me…" Map the answer to the script, connect it to the pricing/spend behavior on the table (`sales-and-pricing.md` underpricing, `finance-fundamentals.md` cash rituals).
- **Scarcity honesty** [contested — report both sides]: the over-borrowing/tunneling effect under scarcity is robust; the "poverty taxes cognitive bandwidth" claim failed an independent replication. Practical carry: a founder in a cash crunch will tunnel on the near-term and over-borrow — that's the documented risk to guard (Foresight tripwires + the funding ladder), without claiming their IQ is impaired.

## 7. The physical foundation — quantified, not preached

- **Sleep**: sleep loss reliably degrades mood (SMD −0.27 to −1.14), raises anxiety (SMD ~0.6), and impairs inhibitory control — the say-no-to-the-dumb-thing function — at g = −0.475 [peer-reviewed meta-analyses]. Coaching move: tie sleep to a specific decision incident the founder regrets, not to wellness talk. "What did you sleep the night before that call?"
- **Exercise** [peer-reviewed, contested after bias correction]: honest framing — reliable for mood and small acute executive-function/reaction-time boosts (g ≈ 0.13–0.36); the "exercise makes you cognitively superior" claim collapses under publication-bias correction (memory ≈ 0.03, EF ≈ null). Prescribe it as mood/energy infrastructure and a boundary-anchor, never as a cognition cure. Never position either as a substitute for clinical treatment.
- Chronic stress physiology: directionally impairing [inference from cited literature — keep claims modest].

## 8. Identity, transitions, and growth

- **Identity fusion is the risk.** High single-role identity predicts worse transition adjustment across the athlete-retirement literature [peer-reviewed scoping review] — the closest analog founders have. Athletes who DIVERSIFIED identity before retiring avoided the crisis [peer-reviewed, prospective]; post-exit trajectories are U-shaped with the trough around 3 months [single trial, n=138]. This is the research behind Kassan's warning in `founder-dossiers.md` ("it'd never been more miserable"). Coaching move — BEFORE any exit, pivot, or failure, not after: "Name three things you are that have nothing to do with this company. If you can't, that's the finding."
- **After failure**: two documented recovery patterns — rebuilding from prior identities vs prolonged letting-go [peer-reviewed, qualitative]. Post-traumatic growth is real for roughly half to two-thirds of people [peer-reviewed, 30-year model], and the mechanism is moving from INTRUSIVE rumination to DELIBERATE meaning-focused reflection. The mentor's line is never "get over it" — it's "we're going to process this on purpose": what the failure proved about your strength, what it opened, what it taught (PTG domains). Stuck in intrusive replay for weeks → referral signal, and the Worry Window (`unblocking-protocols.md`) is the interim tool.

## 9. Referral triggers — armed, conversational, never diagnostic

The boundary rule from `blocker-map.md` holds: coach performance friction inside the business; refer impairment that follows the founder home. Validated brief screens exist (PHQ-2/PHQ-9 for depression, GAD-2/GAD-7 for anxiety) [peer-reviewed clinical instruments] — the mentor NEVER administers them as tests or interprets scores; it uses their two core questions conversationally as referral triggers only ("more days than not in the last two weeks: little interest or pleasure in things? feeling down or hopeless?" — a yes-pattern means saying plainly that a professional is the strong move). Suicidal ideation or harm language: immediate, unambiguous referral, no coaching detour. Honest limit [SOURCE MISSING]: referral itself is an ethics-based standard, not an outcomes-validated intervention — do it anyway.

## Coaching hooks

- "Whose goal is this number — yours, or someone you're still performing for? You'll grind either way; only one of them pays you in a life you want."
- "The research says founder households absorb strain through exactly two channels: your hours and the income floor. Which one is your family absorbing right now?"
- "What do you assume happens if you tell another founder you're struggling? Say the assumption out loud — that belief is more isolating than your calendar is."
- "Finish the sentence: 'If I raise my prices, that makes me…' — now let's look at what that script has been costing you."
- "What did you sleep the night before that decision you regret? The say-no circuit is the first thing sleep loss takes."
- "Name three things you are that have nothing to do with this company. Take your time. If the list is empty, that's this quarter's other project."
