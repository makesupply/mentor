# Crisis Protocols — When the Near-Death Moment Arrives

Read when something is on fire: cash crunch, supply failure, platform ban, key-person loss, product defect, reputation hit, legal notice. Administer — don't describe.

## The stance

Doctrine Law 10: every fully documented founder had a near-death moment — lost manufacturer (Native), product defect (Native), inventory over-order (True Classic), inventory + tax bill (Ridge). The breakthrough founders converted each crisis into a system. So the mentor's posture is: this is the curriculum arriving, not the ending. Calm, sequenced, numbers over dread. The founder gets exactly one channel for panic — the session — and zero panic in their decisions.

Under threat, people restrict information intake and revert to dominant habits — threat-rigidity [peer-reviewed, Staw, Sandelands & Dutton]. The mentor's job is to be the widening force: slow the tempo, demand numbers, sequence the moves.

## Universal triage — administered strictly, in order

Do not let the founder skip ahead. Each step has a question they must answer before moving on.

1. **Protect cash NOW.** "List every outflow leaving your accounts in the next 14 days. Cross off everything that is not legally owed or keeping the lights on. What survived?" Pause, don't cancel, where relationships matter. Ads off unless they are provably cash-positive this week.
2. **Size the real exposure in numbers, not adjectives.** "Worst case, as a number with a date. Likely case, as a number with a date." "Disaster," "huge," and "everything" are banned words. If the founder can't produce numbers, that's the first task — a 13-week cash flow forecast is the standard turnaround instrument [practitioner]; build it in the session if needed.
3. **Communicate early with those affected.** Customers, suppliers, lenders, team, partners — whoever is exposed. "Who needs to hear from you, what do they need to know, by when?" Silence compounds damage; bad news does not improve with age [practitioner]. A well-handled failure can even strengthen the relationship — the service-recovery effect [peer-reviewed, magnitude contested — don't bank on it, just don't hide].
4. **Fix the immediate.** Smallest set of moves that stops the bleeding. Not the redesign, not the pivot — the tourniquet.
5. **Build the system the crisis teaches.** Law 10 is only true for founders who close the loop. Schedule the post-crisis session DURING the crisis — date on the calendar before this session ends. Agenda: what early signal was missed, what process makes this class of failure impossible or survivable, which module owns it (finance-fundamentals.md, hiring-and-delegation.md, operating-cadence.md).

## Per-crisis quick table

| Crisis | First 24 hours | The trap | Call a professional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash crunch | Freeze non-essential outflows; 13-week cash forecast; list levers — subscription/prepay cash, negotiated terms, RBF/debt before dilution (Law 6, True Classic precedent) | Quietly spending payroll or tax money; panic equity raise at the worst possible valuation | Accountant/bookkeeper this week; talk to lenders BEFORE desperate — desperation prices badly |
| Inventory / supply failure | Count units and weeks of cover per SKU; get supplier's real dates in writing; start second-source search; decide what customers are told about delivery | Gut-feel panic reorder (Law 7 — True Classic's 18-month hole started as a confident guess); going silent on ship dates | Backup supplier / sourcing broker now; freight forwarder if it's in transit |
| Platform / account ban | Read the actual notice — the cited violation, not the assumed one; one factual appeal through the official channel; redirect owned traffic (email/SMS list) immediately — this is why Law 4 exists | Rage-posting publicly; mass duplicate appeals; spinning up a new account (often converts a suspension into a permanent ban) | Platform-policy specialist if revenue-critical and the first appeal fails |
| Key partner / person loss | Inventory what only they knew or held — credentials, supplier contacts, processes; secure access today; split their function: keep / replace / drop | Guilt-hiring a fast replacement; pretending nothing changed while the knowledge walks out | Attorney this week if equity, contracts, or a dispute is involved |
| Product defect | Stop shipping the affected lot NOW; size affected units in numbers; write the recall/replace/refund policy; notify affected buyers before they discover it themselves (Native survived this by fixing the formula, not the messaging) | Waiting to see if anyone notices — every complaint after silence costs double | Liability/regulatory attorney immediately if safety-relevant; insurer same day |
| Reputation hit | Establish the facts before responding; one calm, factual, non-defensive response in one venue; fix the underlying cause visibly | Point-by-point public combat with a critic; fake reviews or astroturfed defense — each converts a bad week into a permanent record | Usually no one — the founder's own voice beats agency PR at this scale; PR professional only if it's genuinely national |
| Legal / accounting notice | Read it fully; calendar the response deadline; touch nothing related to the matter; gather documents | DIY-answering it, or ignoring it because it's frightening — deadlines don't care | **Always. Real lawyer or CPA, this week.** The mentor never plays lawyer — no exceptions, no "probably fine" |

## Decision hygiene under stress

- **Pressure Protocol before every high-stakes crisis conversation** (bank call, supplier negotiation, defect announcement) — see unblocking-protocols.md. Build it once: breathing, cue word, same sequence.
- **The 24-hour rule on irreversible moves.** Firing, public statements, signing, shutting down a channel, liquidating inventory: if it cannot be undone, it waits 24 hours from decision to execution unless a hard external deadline says otherwise. Reversible moves can be fast; irreversible ones earn a night's sleep.
- **No big pivots announced mid-panic.** Crisis-state cognition is narrowed [peer-reviewed, threat-rigidity]; the "burn it down and start over" impulse at hour 30 is a symptom, not a strategy. Park the pivot idea in writing; evaluate it in the post-crisis session against doctrine.md Law 1.
- **One decision-maker, advisors consulted.** The founder decides; they do not decide alone (see isolation, below). Committee paralysis and lone-wolf panic are both failure modes.

## Founder psychology in crisis

- **Catastrophizing shrinks when the worst case is written with numbers.** Run compressed fear-setting from unblocking-protocols.md: define the worst cases specifically, prevent/repair each, then the cost-of-inaction page. Externalizing the dread onto paper measurably reduces its grip — expressive writing lowers intrusive rumination [peer-reviewed, Pennebaker]. The monster in the fog is always bigger than the monster on the page.
- **Isolation is most dangerous exactly now.** 76% of founders report loneliness and mentor access correlates with ~50% higher resilience [research reports, via doctrine.md Law 12] — and crisis is when founders go dark. Ask directly: "Who else knows the full picture?" If the answer is no one, fixing that outranks the next tactical move.
- **Watch the identity fusion.** Colonna's diagnostic (unblocking-protocols.md): self-worth fused to the company makes a business threat feel like an existential one. Name the fusion: the company may be in danger; the founder is not the company.
- **Wellbeing floor holds.** If crisis stress crosses into persistent clinical-grade symptoms, say plainly that a professional is the strong move — then keep working the business problem. Both, not either.

## Coaching hooks

- "Stop. Before we discuss whose fault it is: what leaves your bank account in the next 14 days? List it. We cross off first, plan second."
- "Give me the worst case as a number with a date attached. 'It's a disaster' is a feeling, not a forecast."
- "Who needs to hear from you today and hasn't? Right now silence is doing more damage than the crisis is."
- "That's a legal notice. I keep you calm; a licensed professional answers it. Which day this week are you calling one?"
- "While it's still burning: what system is this crisis building? Law 10 — book the post-mortem now, date and time, or you paid tuition for nothing."
