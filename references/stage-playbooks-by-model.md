# Stage Playbooks by Business Model — SaaS, Agency, Marketplace, Creator, Local

Read when the founder's business model (tagged in `state/founder-context.md`) is NOT DTC e-commerce — that vertical's deep playbook stays in `references/stage-playbooks.md`. Condensed from Report 5 (`references/research/report-5-cross-model-benchmarks.md`). B2B structural doctrine lives in `references/b2b-playbook.md`; this file is the NUMBERS layer.

**The provenance rule, enforced hard here:** every benchmark carries source, year, and tier. SaaS and agency numbers rest on named annual surveys [survey data] — quote them with confidence and a date. Marketplace and creator numbers rest mostly on [practitioner claim] aggregators because NO SaaS-Capital-grade survey exists for those verticals — say that limitation out loud when quoting. Local numbers are category-fragmented — bands only. Benchmarks researched Aug 2026; the aging table at the bottom says how fast each number rots.

## B2B SaaS

| Stage | Gate metrics (source · year · tier) |
|---|---|
| $0→$1M ARR | Median time to $1M: ~2yr 9mo, top tier 9mo; top-decile early growth 279% YoY [ChartMogul · 2023 · survey]. Gross margin 55–70% acceptable pre-$500K [practitioner]. CAC payback: no stable benchmark pre-$1M [SOURCE MISSING] — derive the founder's own. |
| $1M→$10M | Growth: top quartile ~70%, median ~30% [ChartMogul · 2023 · survey]. NRR median 100–102% (SaaS Capital says 100–101%, KeyBanc says 102% — different cohorts, quote both) [2024–25 · survey]. GRR median ~91% [SaaS Capital · 2025 · survey]. CAC payback median ~25 months, top quartile 16 — structurally WORSE than 2021's 18 [SaaS Capital · 2024, n=1,520 · survey]. Gross margin working band 65–75% [multiple surveys, ranges conflict]. Magic number median 0.7, top quartile 1.2 [KeyBanc · Oct 2024, n=110 · survey]. |
| $10M+ | Top-quartile growth ~45–49% at $8–15M, decaying toward public medians ~21% [OpenView 2023, ChartMogul 2023 · survey]. NRR >120% earns a ~2.8x valuation-multiple premium [Bessemer · 2024 · investor heuristic]. Pure-SaaS gross margin 80–87% at scale; AI-native runs 55–70% — a structural margin warning [2026 · practitioner/aggregated]. CAC payback compresses to ~9mo top-quartile at $50M+ [SaaS Capital · 2024 · survey]. |

Levers by stage: founder-led sales → repeatable playbook + CS function protecting NRR → expansion revenue, upmarket, international. Deaths: false PMF from friendly deals → leaky bucket masked by new logos, premature sales headcount → deceleration mistaken for softness, AI margin compression. Cash shape: favorable (annual prepay); ARR-backed debt available from ~$1M+.

## Agencies / Professional Services

| Stage | Gate metrics |
|---|---|
| $0→$1M | Utilization/EBITDA benchmarks don't apply to a solo founder [SOURCE MISSING at this scale — say so]. Concentration is 100% by necessity; the exit-ramp target is below. |
| $1M→$10M | Billable utilization: industry average 68.9% (2024) → 66.4% (2025), lowest ever recorded; healthy target 75%+ [SPI Research · 2025, 403 firms · survey]. Firm-wide EBITDA fell 15.4% → 9.8% as utilization dropped [SPI · 2025 · survey] — utilization erosion IS the margin story. Client concentration: practitioner consensus RANGE, never one number — single client >15–30% = danger, top-3 >40–55% [multiple 2025–26 practitioner sources; the spread itself is the finding]. |
| $10M+ | Utilization scales UP with size: 72.4% at 101–300 employees; high-performers 75.4% vs 67.7% for the rest [SPI · 2024–25 · survey]. M&A buyers discount 0.5–2.0x EBITDA when one client exceeds ~15–28% [practitioner]. |

Levers: niche premium + referrals → utilization discipline, retainer mix, deliberate diversification → practice lines, recurring products, roll-up. Deaths: single-client loss with overhead built for it, utilization rot, founder-centric delivery (`b2b-playbook.md` B3/B6 — delegate delivery first, codify judgment). Cash shape: unfavorable (net-30/60/90) — the factoring ladder in `b2b-playbook.md` B8 applies.

## Marketplaces

Weaker evidence base — mostly [practitioner claim]; say so when quoting.

| Stage | Gate metrics |
|---|---|
| $0→$1M | Liquidity BEFORE growth: fill rate ~60% early; >30% of listings transacting in year one = healthy; >40% of buyers repeat within 90 days [2025–26 · practitioner/investor heuristic]. Take rate by category: 2–8% B2B/lead-gen, 8–15% goods, 15–25%+ services [practitioner]. |
| $1M→$10M | Fill rate → ~75%; seller liquidity >60%; scaling median 15% QoQ GMV growth, top decile 35%+ [KnowledgeLib · H2 2025, 200+ marketplaces · survey-ish]. Take rates rising: goods 11%→13% (2024→25), services 18%→20% [same]. Two-sided LTV:CAC bar is ≥4:1, not 3:1 — CAC is paid twice [practitioner]. |
| $10M+ | Fill 85%+; top-quartile take 15–20% approaching profitability; mature growth median 5% QoQ; EV/GMV multiples collapsed 0.65x → 0.25x (2025) [practitioner/survey-ish]. GMV cohort retention >100% = compounding sellers. |

Levers: one narrow niche to liquidity → gradual take-rate increases with trust value-add, supply retention → monetization layers (ads, payments, SaaS tooling). Deaths: GMV vanity with low fill (silent churn), disintermediation in high-take services, category expansion before repeat-transaction proof. Cash: escrow/float models generate cash; pass-through models don't — ask which the founder built.

## Creator / Content / Audience

Weakest survey base of the five [practitioner/aggregated]; platform numbers rot in months.

| Stage | Gate metrics |
|---|---|
| $0→$1M | RPM is niche-destiny: YouTube long-form ~$1.65–$25 by niche (finance/business at the top — 5–10x entertainment); Shorts/TikTok pay 100–300x less per view than long-form [2026 · aggregated]. Newsletter free→paid conversion: sources CONFLICT hard — 0.62% median vs "5% strong" — different denominators; never quote one number without the methodology [2026 · practitioner]. |
| $1M→$10M | Revenue per subscriber $2–5/mo intermediate, $8–15 advanced; sponsorship CPM ~$25–50 at 5–10K subs → $45–80 at 50–100K; engaged-subscriber % ≥35% good [2025–26 · practitioner]. The real KPI is mix: 3+ revenue streams with owned-audience share rising. |
| $10M+ | Functionally a media company; no consolidated survey exists [SOURCE MISSING] — coach on IP licensing, roll-ups, owned distribution, direct sales. |

Levers: high-RPM niche + owned email list FIRST → paid tier, direct brand deals, small team → IP/licensing/media org. Deaths: single-platform algorithm dependence (the Tutorspree lesson at creator scale), scaling fixed costs against platform-dependent revenue, founder-as-brand untransferability. Platform concentration >70–80% of distribution = structural risk [practitioner consensus, no named threshold study — say so].

## Local / Brick-and-Mortar Services

| Stage | Gate metrics |
|---|---|
| $0→$1M | Restaurant labor 25–35% (full-service trending 35–40% under wage pressure); retail labor 10–30% by format; occupancy 8–12% of revenue; restaurant COGS 28–35%, retail 55–65% [2026 · practitioner, Toast/NRF-adjacent]. Combined prime costs can hit 65–80% before admin — the death math. Revenue per location: no cross-category benchmark exists [SOURCE MISSING] — use the founder's trade association. |
| $1M→$10M | Same bands, enforced PER LOCATION with variance analysis; same-store sales growth separates organic from new-unit growth [metric structure, no universal number]. Never expand until location #1's unit economics are systemized and owner-independent. |
| $10M+ | Portfolio-level labor/occupancy variance is the board KPI; franchise vs corporate economics diverge [SOURCE MISSING for standardized comparison — private/FDD data]. |

Levers: unit economics + local flywheel + owner-independent SOPs → replication discipline, centralized purchasing, regional managers → franchising/density/vertical integration. Deaths: over-leasing early, replicating an unproven model, labor-band squeeze from wage legislation, quality dilution at footprint scale. Cash: daily settlement (the best cash shape in this file) but capex-lumpy per new unit; SBA/equipment financing is the norm, equity is rare.

## Cross-model translation table — same concept, native metric

Use this to translate a principle across verticals WITHOUT borrowing a neighbor's number:

| Concept | SaaS | Agency | Marketplace | Creator | Local |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retention | NRR ~100–102% / GRR ~91% [survey] | Client repeat-engagement [SOURCE MISSING] | Seller 12-mo retention >50%; buyer repeat >40%/90d | Engaged-subscriber % 35–50%+ | Repeat rate / same-store sales [no universal #] |
| Unit economics | CAC payback (16–25mo) · LTV:CAC 3–5:1 | Utilization × rate − loaded cost | Take rate net of dual-sided CAC · ≥4:1 | RPM / revenue-per-subscriber | Contribution per location after labor+COGS+occupancy |
| Concentration risk | Customer >15% [practitioner] | Client >15–30% / top-3 >40–55% | Single supply partner or demand channel [no #] | Single platform >70–80% [no named study] | Single location/supplier/anchor [no #] |
| Cash conversion | Favorable (prepay) | Unfavorable (net-30/90) | Escrow=float vs pass-through=nothing | Lumpy net-30/45 payouts | Daily cash, capex-lumpy |
| Margin health | Gross margin 65–85% by tier | EBITDA 9.8–15.4% firm-wide | Take rate net of costs 5–30% | Revenue-mix diversification [no single margin #] | Prime cost (labor+COGS) 55–70% |

## Aging velocity — how fast each number rots

Fastest to slowest: (1) SaaS CAC payback and creator RPM/per-view rates — months; (2) agency utilization — one fiscal year; (3) marketplace take rates and EV/GMV multiples — one year; (4) local labor % — moves with wage legislation; (5) concentration thresholds and occupancy % — structural, slow. Timestamp every benchmark quoted; anything pre-2024 gets re-verified before a decision hangs on it.

## Coaching hooks

- "That's a SaaS number and you run an agency. Your retention metric is repeat engagements, and your margin story is utilization — which was 66% industry-wide last year and falling. What's yours?"
- "Your GMV headline is up and your fill rate is 55%. Half your buyers are leaving empty-handed and churning silently. Liquidity before growth — which niche gets to 75% first?"
- "Finance content pays five to ten times entertainment per view. Before we optimize your hooks: are you sure about the niche?"
- "Your labor is 38% and your rent is 13%. The bands say that location dies slowly no matter how good the product is. Renegotiate one of those numbers or don't open location two."
- "I can give you a benchmark for that, but it's a practitioner aggregate, not a real survey — no one runs SaaS-Capital-grade research on your vertical. Better plan: your own trailing baseline, starting today."
