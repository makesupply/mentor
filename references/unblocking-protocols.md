# Unblocking Protocols — Block Table + Intervention Scripts

Condensed from Report 3 (`references/research/report-3-psychology-coaching.md`). Administer these — walk the founder through each step and make them answer. Never just describe a tool.

## Block table

| Mental block | Intervention | The mentor's opening line |
|---|---|---|
| Fear of shipping / launching | Implementation intentions [peer-reviewed] | "If the launch fails, what exactly happens Tuesday morning? Name it. Now: what's the if-then plan for that specific obstacle?" |
| Perfectionism / endless iteration | WOOP — force naming the INTERNAL obstacle [peer-reviewed] | "What's the real obstacle — the product, or you needing to feel ready? Pick the internal one." |
| Decision paralysis on a big bet | Fear-setting [practitioner] | "Write the worst case. Now write the cost of doing nothing for 12 months. Which number is scarier?" |
| Avoiding a hard sales/pricing conversation | Feedforward [practitioner, Goldsmith] | "Stop explaining why the last one failed. Give me two things you'll do differently on the next call." |
| Isolation / deciding alone | Mentor/peer access → +50% resilience [research report] | "Who did you talk to about this before deciding? If the answer is no one, that's the actual problem." |
| Burnout / no boundaries | Boundary-setting: 45% vs 6% low burnout [research] | "What did you NOT do this week because you protected it? If nothing, you don't have boundaries, you have a slogan." |
| Shiny-object syndrome / channel- or venture-hopping | Radical self-inquiry + one-behavior commitment [practitioner] | "How have you been complicit in creating this scattered pipeline? Pick one. What's the if-then for staying on it 90 days?" |
| Underpricing out of fear of rejection | Mental contrasting [peer-reviewed] | "What's the actual fear — losing the sale, or hearing no to your face? Say it out loud." |
| Team/hire/delegation avoidance | Campbell: work the team, then the problem [practitioner] | "Is this a hiring problem or a trust problem? Work the team first." |
| Co-founder / partner conflict | Campbell's work-the-team + Colonna's unsaid-things questions [practitioner] | "What are you not saying to your co-founder that needs to be said? Say it to me first, then we'll script how you say it to them." |

Note: "founder perfectionism," "shiny-object syndrome," and "sales avoidance" lack founder-specific peer-reviewed constructs [SOURCE MISSING] — the interventions rest on adjacent validated psychology. Say so if it matters.

## Scripts

### If-Then Builder (implementation intentions)
Effect: medium-to-large on goal attainment [Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta-analysis, peer-reviewed]. Steps: (1) One goal-directed action. (2) One concrete situational cue — time, place, or trigger event. (3) Bind: "If [cue], then I [action]." Reject vague cues ("this week") — demand "Tuesday 9am" or "when X happens." Every session commitment ships in this format.

### WOOP (mental contrasting + implementation intentions)
[Peer-reviewed, Oettingen/Gollwitzer.] Administer in strict order, one answer at a time:
1. **Wish** — one sentence, feasible-but-challenging.
2. **Outcome** — best result, vividly imagined for a moment.
3. **Obstacle** — the INTERNAL obstacle (emotion, belief, habit). Push past external excuses.
4. **Plan** — "If [obstacle appears], then I [action]."
Warning to state: pure positive visualization without steps 3–4 measurably REDUCES motivation. Never let the founder stop at step 2.

### Fear-Setting (Ferriss — practitioner, Stoic premeditatio malorum)
Three pages, then the twist:
1. **Define** the worst cases (10–20, specific). For each: **Prevent** (reduce likelihood) and **Repair** (recover if it happens).
2. **Benefits** of an attempt or partial success.
3. **Cost of inaction** — at 6 months, 1 year, 3 years. Financially, emotionally, physically.
Page 3 is where decisions actually flip. Make them write it, not think it.

### Feedforward (Goldsmith)
Rules: pick ONE behavior to change; ask for TWO future-facing suggestions (no critique of the past); respond only "thank you" — no judging, no debating, no "but" (say "and"). The mentor gives feedforward the same way: two next-time actions, zero autopsy.

### Radical Self-Inquiry (Colonna)
The signature question: **"How have I been complicit in creating the conditions I say I don't want?"** — deliver with curiosity, not accusation. The three deeper questions: What am I not saying that needs to be said? What's being said that I'm not hearing? What am I saying that's not being heard? Colonna's diagnostic for high-achievers: self-worth fused to goal achievement makes uncertainty feel like identity threat — name that fusion when you see it.

### The Campbell Kit
- **Coachability filter:** honesty, humility, work ethic, perseverance, openness. Only coach the coachable — if the founder is deflecting, name it before proceeding.
- **Work the team, then the problem** — diagnose the relationship/structure before the substantive issue (for a founder running AI agents, this includes the AI-agent "team").
- **Five-topic 1:1:** Personal / Peer relationships / Performance with metrics / Management-leadership / Innovation.
- **Free-form listening:** full attention, questions that surface the real issue.
- **Five words:** both parties write five words for what matters most; merge into the agenda.
- **No gap between statements and facts:** blunt, candid, private, and visibly caring.

### Pressure Protocol (pre-performance routine)
[Peer-reviewed meta-analysis: g = 0.31 pre-post; 0.64–0.70 in controlled designs, holding under high pressure.] Before high-stakes moments (launch day, big call, negotiation): (1) slow breathing, (2) one task-relevant cue word, (3) the same short sequence every time. Build it once with the founder, reuse forever.

### Deload Cadence [inference — sport periodization applied to founder work]
Athletes deload ~6.4 days every ~5.6 weeks to manage fatigue. Application: after any 4–6-week heavy push, schedule a deliberately lighter week (maintenance work, no new initiatives). Label it periodization, not slacking — the data says planned recovery precedes the next performance block.

### The Directness Governor
[Peer-reviewed + review literature.] Candor improves performance (γ = 0.50 study; care and challenge correlate r = 0.61 — they are allies, not tradeoffs) ONLY on a foundation of demonstrated care and consent. Consent is established explicitly at Intake and recorded as the directness calibration in `state/founder-context.md` — honor the intensity the founder chose, and revisit it rather than assume it. Demonstrate care through the ledger, memory, and specificity. If trust feels thin in a session, repair first, challenge second. Directness without relationship is just criticism.

### Wellbeing Floor
93% of founders show mental-health strain; 76% report loneliness; anxiety runs ~5x national average [research reports]. This skill is part of the mitigation — mentor access correlates with +50% resilience — but it is not therapy. Persistent clinical-grade symptoms → say plainly that a professional is the strong move, then keep supporting the business.
